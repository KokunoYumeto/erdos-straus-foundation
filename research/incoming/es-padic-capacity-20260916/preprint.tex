\title{A power saving for available Erd\H{o}s--Straus factor blocks}
\author{The Clankers}

\begin{abstract}
We specialize an explicit two-$2$-adic-logarithm theorem to obtain a uniform
power saving in the exponent capacity of the actual $3,11$ factor block
modulo $2^k$. The onset is very large. Exact lattice rounding returns bounded
words, and relative exponent division preserves the original heights. The
application improves a proved $O(2^k/k)$ sufficient budget, not universal ES
occupancy. The logarithmic theorem is imported with its numerical parameters;
no historical-priority claim is made.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Original arithmetic.}
Fix $k\ge3$, $u=2^{2k-5}$, $p\equiv1\pmod8$, $p>2u$, and retain
$N=p+4u=3^e11^fM$, $\gcd(M,33)=1$.
An original middle state is equivalent to
\[
 Q\mid N,\quad Q\equiv-1\pmod{2^k},\qquad R=N/Q,
 \quad a=(p+R)/4.                                         \tag{1}
\]
Indeed $u\mid a^2\iff2^{k-2}\mid a$. The target gives
$R\equiv-p\pmod{2^k}$, so this holds. Both $Q,R\ge3$, and $N<3p$ gives
$R,Q<p$. The middle gate is $R\mid a+u$. The implications reverse.
With $d_0=\gcd(a,u)$, set
\[
 (h,r,s)=(d_0^2/u,u/d_0,a/d_0),\quad\lambda=(r+s)/R,
 \quad(x,y,z)=(a,phs\lambda,phr\lambda).
\]
Prime valuations prove integrality and $a=hrs,u=hr^2,\gcd(r,s)=1$.
Clearing reciprocals gives $4/p=1/x+1/y+1/z$, with ordered inverse
$u=pa^2/(Ry-pa)$. These are inherited Type II coordinates \cite{ET}.
Integer $p$ is permitted; prime examples require their own primality proof.

\paragraph{The exact relation lattice.}
Let $o=2^{k-2}$. Difference-of-squares induction gives
$v_2(3^{2^t}-1)=t+2$, hence
$H_k=\langle3\rangle=\{x\equiv1,3\pmod8\}$ has order $o$.
Retain the unique $0\le J_k<o$ with $3^{J_k}\equiv11\pmod{2^k}$ and put
\[
 \mathcal L_k=\mathbb Z(o,0)+\mathbb Z(-J_k,1)
 =\{(a,b):3^a11^b\equiv1\pmod{2^k}\}.                    \tag{2}
\]
Its determinant is $o$. Nonzero $(a,b)$ makes $3^a11^b-1\ne0$ by prime
factorization, even when one or both exponents are negative.

\begin{lemma}[Explicit logarithmic specialization]
For either triple
\[
 (C,H,D_0)=(550000,140,400000)
 \quad\text{or}\quad(387000,4000,270000),                  \tag{3}
\]
every nonzero integer pair, with $B=\max(|a|,|b|)$, satisfies
\[
 v_2(3^a11^b-1)<C\max\{\log B,H\}.
\]
Consequently, for $k\ge CH$, every nonzero member of $\mathcal L_k$ satisfies
$B>\exp(k/C)>2^{\lfloor k/D_0\rfloor}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use Chim \cite[Theorem 2.1; Table 1 and formulas, pp.~346--348]{Chim},
at local prime 2 and rational field parameters $D=e=f=g=1$, $\kappa=2$,
$Z_1=4$. For $ab\ne0$, set $\alpha_1=3^{\operatorname{sgn}a}$,
$\alpha_2=11^{-\operatorname{sgn}b}$ and $b_1=|a|,b_2=|b|$.
Its nonzero form is $11^{-b}(3^a11^b-1)$, with the same valuation. The original
heights are $\log3,\log11$. Put $b'=b_1/\log11+b_2/\log3$.
For the two respective table rows
\[
 (x_1,x_2,x_3,C_2)=(200,25,4.93,5500),
 \quad(5000,900,4.85,3920),
\]
the source bound is
\[
 v_2(\Lambda)<\frac{2C_2(2\log2+x_3)\log3\log11}{(\log2)^3}
 \max\{\log(b'\log2),x_1\log2,x_2(2\log2+x_3)\log2\}.
\]
The rational enclosures
$.6931<\log2<.6932$, $1.0986<\log3<1.0987$,
$2.3978<\log11<2.3980$ imply, respectively, coefficient bounds
$549798<C$, $386893<C$, fixed maxima below $H$, and $C\log2<D_0$.
They also give $\log2(1/\log11+1/\log3)<1$, so the variable term is below
$\log B$. These enclosures follow from twelve terms of
$\log x=2\sum_{j\ge0}((x-1)/(x+1))^{2j+1}/(2j+1)$ with its geometric tail,
using $x=2,3/2,11/8$.
On either axis the valuation is 1 for odd exponent and $2+v_2(B)$ for even
exponent, which satisfies (3) directly. If a relation has $k\ge CH$, its
valuation at least $k$ forces $\log B>k/C$. This proves the conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform bounded-word power saving]
For $k\ge77{,}000{,}000$, every element of $H_k$ is $3^i11^j$ with
\[
 0\le i,j\le 2^{k-1-\lfloor k/400000\rfloor}.
\]
For $k\ge1{,}548{,}000{,}000$, the stronger bound is
\[
 \boxed{0\le i,j\le T(k):=2^{k-1-\lfloor k/270000\rfloor}.} \tag{4}
\]
In particular the second sufficient square side is
$O((2^k)^{1-1/270000})$. Every word has an exact constructive return.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take an exact Gauss-reduced basis $b_1,b_2$ of (2), with
$\lambda=\|b_1\|\le\|b_2\|$ and
$|\langle b_1,b_2\rangle|\le\lambda^2/2$ \cite[\S17.1]{Galbraith}.
Writing $b_2=\mu b_1+h e_\perp$ gives $|\mu|\le1/2$,
$h=o/\lambda$, and $h^2\ge3\lambda^2/4$.
A lattice vector with second coefficient zero has length at least $\lambda$;
with coefficient $\pm1$ the reduced basis gives length at least $\|b_2\|$;
with larger coefficient its perpendicular length is at least $2h>\lambda$.
Thus $\lambda$ is shortest and $\lambda<4\sqrt o/3$.

Let $D_0$ correspond to the applicable row of (3), $U=2^{\lfloor k/D_0\rfloor}$,
and $T=2o/U$. The lemma gives $\lambda>U$. For target $t$, put $v=(t,0)$,
$c=(T/2,T/2)$ and $B_0=(b_1\ b_2)$. The integer word
\[
 \boxed{w=v+B_0\operatorname{round}(B_0^{-1}(c-v))}          \tag{5}
\]
has the desired residue. Nearest-integer coefficient errors are at most $1/2$,
so its distance from $c$ is at most
\[
 \frac12(\|b_1\|+\|b_2\|)
 \le\frac34\lambda+\frac{o}{2\lambda}
 <\sqrt o+\frac{o}{2U}\le\frac T2.
\]
For the stated ranges $4\lfloor k/D_0\rfloor\le k-2$ and
$\lfloor k/D_0\rfloor\ge1$, which implies $\sqrt o\le T/4$; the other
term is $T/4$. Therefore both integer coordinates lie in $[0,T]$.
Finally $T\le(2^k)^{1-1/D_0}$.
\end{proof}

The complete word fibre is $((t,0)+\mathcal L_k)\cap([0,e]\times[0,f])$.
Its inverse basis coordinates are $B_0^{-1}(w-(t,0))\in\mathbb Z^2$.
All rounding ties are fixed towards $+\infty$; rounding is not claimed to solve
closest-vector minimization \cite[\S18.2]{Galbraith}.
At any precision the exact row-sum widths
\[
 W_3=|b_{1,1}|+|b_{2,1}|,\qquad W_{11}=|b_{1,2}|+|b_{2,2}|
\]
give an often smaller rectangle, by the same coordinate rounding argument.
The former uniform estimate used only $\lambda>k/4$, yielding
$\max\{4\lceil\sqrt o\rceil,\lceil8o/k\rceil\}$.
The new power bound is an asymptotic improvement with an enormous onset,
not a new practical census result or an optimized numerical constant.

\paragraph{Original ES implication and mass.}
If the actual exponents $e,f$ in (1) meet the stated sufficient side, an original
middle state exists exactly when $M$ has a prime factor $5$ or $7\pmod8$.
Necessity holds because all other factors lie in $H_k$.
For an actual such prime $q$, apply (5) at
$t=\log_3(-q^{-1})$; then $Q=3^i11^jq\mid N$ returns (1) and its ordered
positive denominators. No unavailable exponent is introduced.
If $A(M)$ counts all outside divisors of $M$, disjoint translated rectangles give
\[
 \boxed{C_k(p)\ge A(M)
 \left\lfloor\frac{e+1}{T+1}\right\rfloor
 \left\lfloor\frac{f+1}{T+1}\right\rfloor.}                \tag{6}
\]
Distinct $i,j,t$ give distinct integer cofactors by unique factorization.
The inventory requirement is substantial: $e,f\ge T$ and an outside prime
imply $3p>N\ge5\cdot33^T$. Nothing here asserts that every prime supplies it.

\paragraph{Relative precision, with no inflated logarithmic heights.}
Fix $d=2^v$, $h=k-v\ge3$. The map
\[
 H_h\xrightarrow{\sim}\langle3^d\rangle\subset H_k,
 \qquad 3^a\longmapsto3^{da}
\]
is well-defined and has inverse exponent division. It also sends $11$ to
$11^d$, since $J_k\bmod2^{h-2}=J_h$. Consequently
\[
 \boxed{3^{da}11^{db}\equiv1\pmod{2^k}
 \iff3^a11^b\equiv1\pmod{2^h}.}                           \tag{7}
\]
The higher-layer relation lattice is exactly $\mathcal L_h$. There is no need
to apply the logarithmic theorem to the inflated heights of $3^d$ and $11^d$.

The actual digit decomposition is
\[
 i=r+dI,\quad j=s+dJ,\quad0\le r,s<d,
 \quad I\le\lfloor(e-r)/d\rfloor,
 \quad J\le\lfloor(f-s)/d\rfloor.
\]
Retain only available lower digits with $t-r-J_ks\equiv0\pmod d$.
Their higher target is $(t-r-J_ks)/d$ modulo $2^{h-2}$ and their higher
step is $J_h$. The power bound at precision $h$, or its exact finite basis
widths, now gives an original word. Euclidean division recovers every digit
and every capacity; different digit fibres are disjoint.

The associated relative boundary remains the explicit module comparison
\[
 \iota_d:\mathbb F_2[C_d]\xrightarrow{\sim}\ker((X+1)^d),
 \qquad X^r\longmapsto X^r\sum_{j=0}^{o/d-1}X^{dj}.
\]
Its image consists of vectors constant on those cosets. Reading one coefficient
per coset after that image test is its inverse; ordinary quotient summation
has $\pi_d\iota_d=(o/d)I=0$. This is the prior SplitZero BR1 specialization
\cite{SplitZero}, not a new algebra-inverse assertion. Integer preimage counts
and support labels remain before reduction. A receiving supported zero
$e=0_A^\bullet$ is not absence $\tau$. The new coverage theorem does not claim
its entire counting packet is this uniform relative norm.

\paragraph{Finite certificate and limits.}
At the inherited prime $p=482187176641$, $k=6$,
$N=3^7 11^5 37^2$, the exact basis $(2,2),(-5,3)$ has determinant 16 and
widths $(7,5)$. The target for the actual outside divisor 37 is 13. Formula
(5) returns $(i,j)=(1,4)$, hence
\[
 (Q,R,a,h,r,s,\lambda)
 =(1625151,296703,120546868336,2,8,7534179271,25393),
\]
\[
 (x,y,z)=(120546868336,184499678869490521595996846,195906863623118608).
\]
The complete target fibre is $(1,4),(6,1)$. Primality is checked by full
trial division. This known branch illustrates the exact return, not the
large-onset asymptotic improvement.

At $k=8,v=2$, the integer $N=3^{28}11^{20}31^2$ has an available lower
fibre $(r,s)=(0,0)$. The precision-six basis returns $(I,J)=(5,3)$,
hence the original $(i,j)=(20,12)$ and $Q=3^{20}11^{12}31$.
Primality of $p=N-8192$ is not asserted.

The standard-library package verifies all 2046 cyclic targets through $k=12$,
258 full basis certificates through $k=4096$, complete bounded fibres,
signed/axis cases, and relative inverses. A separate implementation checks
modular powers and original divisor returns without importing the first.
No giant threshold modulus was computed. The imported logarithmic theorem,
not a bounded experiment, supplies the universal estimate. No new Lean build,
independent mathematical review, historical priority or universal ES theorem
is claimed.
