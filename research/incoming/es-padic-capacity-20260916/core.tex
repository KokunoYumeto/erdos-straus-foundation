\section{The arithmetic estimate selected for continuation}
The prior two-block tranche proved a uniform exponent budget $O(2^k/k)$ and
isolated the nonzero $2$-adic form which would improve it
\cite[Theorem 3, (15)--(20)]{PriorTwo}. The relative-boundary tranche then
showed how actual high-factor blocks lift a retained lower residue
\cite[Theorem 2, (C3)--(C7)]{PriorRelative}. Here we supply the missing
quantitative estimate, using a primary-source two-logarithm theorem, and
transport it through that exact relative decomposition.

The result is an explicit power saving, not merely a smaller multiplicative
constant. Its onset is enormous and it is not a practical improvement in the
previous two-million-prime census. The exact finite basis certificates remain
much better in that range. No universal occupancy, historical priority, or
independent proof of the imported logarithmic theorem is asserted.

\section{Original states and their complete ordered return}
Fix
\[
 k\ge3,\quad u=2^{2k-5},\quad p\equiv1\pmod8,\quad p>2u,
 \quad N=p+4u=3^e11^fM,\quad\gcd(M,33)=1.                 \tag{A1}
\]
The structural statements allow integer $p$; primality is separately checked
in the prime example. Original middle states at this seed are in bijection with
\[
 Q\mid N,\quad Q\equiv-1\pmod{2^k},\qquad
 R=N/Q,\quad a=(p+R)/4.                                  \tag{A2}
\]
Indeed $K(u)=2^{k-2}$ satisfies $u\mid a^2\iff K(u)\mid a$.
Since $N\equiv p\pmod{2^k}$, the target gives $R\equiv-p\pmod{2^k}$
and hence this availability. Both $Q,R$ are $3\pmod4$ and at least three.
The inequality $N<3p$ gives $Q,R<p$. Also $4(a+u)=R+N$ gives
$R\mid a+u$. All implications reverse. Since $u$ is a power of two,
$\gcd(p,N)=1$ even without assuming $p$ prime.

Retain
\[
 d_0=\gcd(a,u),\quad(h,r,s)=(d_0^2/u,u/d_0,a/d_0),\quad
 \lambda=(r+s)/R,\quad(x,y,z)=(a,phs\lambda,phr\lambda).    \tag{A3}
\]
Prime valuations give $a=hrs$, $u=hr^2$, $\gcd(r,s)=1$ and integer
$h,r,s$. All are units modulo $R$, so $R\mid a+u=hr(r+s)$ makes
$\lambda$ integral. Clearing the reciprocal sum gives
$p\lambda+r+s=(p+R)\lambda=4hrs\lambda$. The complete ordered inverse is
\[
 \boxed{u=\frac{pa^2}{Ry-pa}.}                             \tag{A4}
\]
These are inherited Type II coordinates \cite[\S2, (2.13)--(2.22)]{ET}.
The factorization of $N$ is input, not the returned factorization of $a$.

Put $o_k=2^{k-2}$. The order identity
$v_2(3^{2^t}-1)=t+2$ for $t\ge1$ proves
\[
 H_k=\langle3\rangle=\{w:w\equiv1,3\pmod8\}
 \subset(\mathbb Z/2^k\mathbb Z)^\times,\qquad |H_k|=o_k.
\]
Let $0\le J_k<o_k$ be the unique logarithm $3^{J_k}\equiv11$.
It is odd. The exact relation lattice is
\[
 \mathcal L_k=\{(a,b)\in\mathbb Z^2:a+J_kb\equiv0\pmod{o_k}\}
 =\mathbb Z(o_k,0)+\mathbb Z(-J_k,1),\quad\det\mathcal L_k=o_k.
                                                               \tag{A5}
\]
Its congruence is $3^a11^b\equiv1\pmod{2^k}$. A modular relation is not
an equality of positive integer products. Unique factorization makes
$3^a11^b-1$ nonzero whenever $(a,b)\ne(0,0)$.

\section{An explicit imported logarithmic estimate, with every parameter}
We use Chim \cite[Theorem 2.1; \S10, Table 1 and final formula on
pp.~346--348]{Chim}. The local prime in that theorem is $2$, not the ES
parameter $p$. For rational $2$-adic units the field parameters are
$D=e=f=g=1$, $\kappa=2$, $Z_1=4$. Take $A_1=3,A_2=11$.
They meet the prescribed logarithmic-height lower bounds, also when either
rational input is inverted.

For a row $(x_1,x_2,x_3,C_2)$ of the $p=2$ block of Chim's table, set
\[
 \mathcal K=\frac{2C_2(2\log2+x_3)\log3\log11}{(\log2)^3},\quad
 \mathcal H=\max\!\left\{\log b'+\log\log2,
 x_1\log2,x_2(2\log2+x_3)\log2\right\},
\]
where $b'=b_1/\log11+b_2/\log3$. The cited theorem gives
$v_2(\alpha_1^{b_1}-\alpha_2^{b_2})<\mathcal K\mathcal H$ for
positive $b_1,b_2$ and multiplicatively independent inputs. We use two rows:
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrr|rrr}
 &x_1&x_2&x_3&C_2&C&H&D_0\\\hline
 A&200&25&4.93&5500&550000&140&400000\\
 B&5000&900&4.85&3920&387000&4000&270000
\end{array}                                                   \tag{B1}
\]
The last three columns are our rationally certified roundings, not constants
quoted verbatim from the source.

\begin{theorem}[Nonzero relation height]\label{thm:height}
For either row of (B1), every $(a,b)\in\mathbb Z^2\setminus\{(0,0)\}$,
with $B=\max(|a|,|b|)$, satisfies
\[
 \boxed{v_2(3^a11^b-1)<C\max\{\log B,H\}.}                 \tag{B2}
\]
If $k\ge CH$ and $(a,b)\in\mathcal L_k\setminus\{0\}$, then
\[
 \boxed{B>\exp(k/C)>2^{\lfloor k/D_0\rfloor}.}             \tag{B3}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $ab\ne0$, take
$\alpha_1=3^{\operatorname{sgn}a}$,
$\alpha_2=11^{-\operatorname{sgn}b}$,
$b_1=|a|$, $b_2=|b|$. Then
\[
 \alpha_1^{b_1}-\alpha_2^{b_2}=11^{-b}(3^a11^b-1).
\]
The multiplier is a $2$-adic unit, so the valuations agree. Both heights and
all field parameters stated above apply with either sign; the inputs are
multiplicatively independent by their distinct prime valuations.

The following rigorous enclosures are sufficient:
\[
 .6931<\log2<.6932,\quad1.0986<\log3<1.0987,\quad
 2.3978<\log11<2.3980.                                    \tag{B4}
\]
They imply
$\log2(1/\log11+1/\log3)<1$, and hence
$\log b'+\log\log2<\log B$.
For row A they give $\mathcal K<549798<550000$ and both fixed terms of
$\mathcal H$ below $140$; for row B, $\mathcal K<386893<387000$ and both
fixed terms below $4000$. They also give $C\log2<D_0$ in both rows.
The exact rational upper expressions, rather than rounded decimals, are
recorded and checked in \texttt{constants.json}.

For completeness (B4) follows without numerical logarithm software from
\[
 \log x=2\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{z^{2j+1}}{2j+1}+R_n,
 \quad0<R_n\le\frac{2z^{2n+1}}{(2n+1)(1-z^2)},\quad z=\frac{x-1}{x+1}.
\]
Use $n=12$ for $x=2,3/2,11/8$, with
$\log3=\log2+\log(3/2)$ and $\log11=3\log2+\log(11/8)$.
This proves the displayed finite roundings.

If $a=0$ or $b=0$, the valuation is $1$ for an odd nonzero exponent, and
$2+v_2(B)$ for an even one, by difference-of-squares induction. The same holds
for inverse powers. These quantities satisfy (B2) directly; the imported theorem
has not been applied to an exponent zero.

Finally a relation has valuation at least $k$. If $\log B\le H$, (B2)
would give $k<CH$, impossible. Otherwise $k<C\log B$.
Since $C\log2<D_0$, exponentiation proves (B3).
\end{proof}

This is a specialization of the cited logarithmic theorem, not a new generic
Baker-type theorem. Linear dependence on $\log B$ is what allows a power
saving below; a quadratic dependence would give a different rate.

\section{From short-relation exclusion to bounded original words}
Let $b_1,b_2$ be an exact Gauss-reduced basis of $\mathcal L_k$:
\[
 \lambda=\|b_1\|\le\|b_2\|,\qquad
 |\langle b_1,b_2\rangle|\le\lambda^2/2.
\]
It is obtained by integer swaps and nearest-integer subtractions; termination
uses decreasing positive squared norms, with a stop on equality to avoid a
rounding-tie cycle. These are the classical reduction operations
\cite[\S17.1, Theorem 17.1.2 and Algorithm 23]{Galbraith}.

Here are the precise estimates used. Write
$b_2=\mu b_1+h e_\perp$, so $|\mu|\le1/2$, $h=o_k/\lambda$, and
$h^2\ge3\lambda^2/4$. A vector with second coefficient zero has length at
least $\lambda$. For second coefficient $\pm1$, reducedness gives length
at least $\|b_2\|$. For absolute coefficient at least two, its perpendicular
part has length at least $2h>\lambda$. Thus $b_1$ is shortest. Also
\[
 \lambda^2\le\frac{2o_k}{\sqrt3},\qquad
 \lambda<\frac43\sqrt{o_k},\qquad
 \frac12(\|b_1\|+\|b_2\|)
 \le\frac34\lambda+\frac{o_k}{2\lambda}.                  \tag{C1}
\]

For a target $t\in\mathbb Z/o_k$ and a rectangle
$[a,a+E]\times[b,b+F]$, let $c=(a+E/2,b+F/2)$, $v=(t,0)$, and let
$B_0$ have columns $b_1,b_2$. The actual returned word is
\[
 \boxed{w=v+B_0\operatorname{round}(B_0^{-1}(c-v)).}        \tag{C2}
\]
Rounding is coordinatewise nearest integer, ties towards $+\infty$.
It is not asserted to give a closest vector. Its coordinate errors are at
most $1/2$, so (C1) bounds its Euclidean displacement from $c$.
The exact basis-dependent sufficient widths are
\[
 W_3=|b_{1,1}|+|b_{2,1}|,\qquad
 W_{11}=|b_{1,2}|+|b_{2,2}|.                               \tag{C3}
\]
They need no logarithmic theorem. If $E\ge W_3,F\ge W_{11}$, (C2) lies in
the original rectangle. Its complete fibre is
$(v+\mathcal L_k)\cap([a,a+E]\times[b,b+F])$; the inverse on any such word
is $B_0^{-1}(w-v)\in\mathbb Z^2$. The subtractions, translations and rounding
convention are retained, not projected away \cite[\S18.2]{Galbraith}.

\begin{theorem}[Explicit uniform power-saving capacity]\label{thm:capacity}
For either row $(C,H,D_0)$ of (B1), suppose $k\ge CH$. Put
\[
 \boxed{T_{D_0}(k)=2^{\,k-1-\lfloor k/D_0\rfloor}.}        \tag{C4}
\]
Every cyclic target has an original word $3^i11^j$, $0\le i,j\le T_{D_0}(k)$.
The word has the terminating exact construction (C2). In particular,
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 k\text{ range}&\text{sufficient square side}&\text{rate}\\\hline
 k\ge77{,}000{,}000&T_{400000}(k)&O((2^k)^{1-1/400000})\\
 k\ge1{,}548{,}000{,}000&T_{270000}(k)&O((2^k)^{1-1/270000})
\end{array}                                                   \tag{C5}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:height}, $\lambda>U=2^{\lfloor k/D_0\rfloor}$.
Equation (C1) bounds the rounding displacement strictly by
$\sqrt{o_k}+o_k/(2U)$. Let $T=2o_k/U$, which is (C4).
For the stated $k$, $4\lfloor k/D_0\rfloor\le k-2$, so
$\sqrt{o_k}/U\ge2$; consequently $\sqrt{o_k}\le T/4$ and
$o_k/(2U)=T/4$. The error is strictly less than $T/2$.
This places the returned integer coordinates in the square centred at $(T/2,T/2)$.
Finally $\lfloor k/D_0\rfloor\ge k/D_0-1$ gives
$T\le(2^k)^{1-1/D_0}$.
\end{proof}

The prior estimate is
$\max\{4\lceil\sqrt{o_k}\rceil,\lceil8o_k/k\rceil\}$.
At the stated onsets, $2^{\lfloor k/D_0\rfloor}>k/4$; this remains true in
successive length-$D_0$ buckets because the left side doubles while the
right endpoint grows linearly. Therefore (C4) is strictly below its second
term. Below the large onsets retain the prior bound or the elementary side
$o_k-1$. At any precision, the exact widths (C3) may be much smaller than
any uniform bound. No threshold modulus in (C5) was explicitly expanded in
the finite computation.

\paragraph{Consequences for actual ES input.}
If the actual exponents in (A1) satisfy $e,f\ge T_{D_0}(k)$, then
\[
 \boxed{C_k(p)>0\iff M\text{ has a prime divisor }5\text{ or }7\pmod8.}
                                                               \tag{C6}
\]
Necessity holds because products of only residues $1,3\pmod8$ cannot be $-1$.
For sufficiency, take such an actual prime $q$ and target
$t=\log_3(-q^{-1})\pmod{o_k}$. The bounded word returns
$Q=3^i11^jq\mid N$, then (A2)--(A4). No primality of a selected residue or
factorization oracle is inferred from the logarithmic estimate.

Let $A(M)$ count all outside divisors of $M$. A sufficient integer side $T$
also gives the exact original-state lower bound
\[
 C_k(p)\ge A(M)
 \left\lfloor\frac{e+1}{T+1}\right\rfloor
 \left\lfloor\frac{f+1}{T+1}\right\rfloor.                 \tag{C7}
\]
Partition the exponent intervals into disjoint $(T+1)$-point intervals.
Every translated rectangle covers the cyclic group by (C2). Unique prime
factorization separates its different $i,j,t$, so no original state is counted
twice. Smaller basis-dependent rectangles may be used instead.
The capacity input is strong: whenever $e,f\ge T$ and an outside factor exists,
$3p>N\ge5\cdot33^T$. This precludes presenting (C5) as a practical small-$p$ test.

\section{Relative boundary transport without increasing the heights}
Fix $v\ge0$, $d=2^v$, and $h=k-v\ge3$. The actual group map
\[
 H_h\longrightarrow H_k,\qquad g\longmapsto g^d             \tag{D1}
\]
is well-defined, injective, and has image $\langle3^d\rangle$. It is a
map between the indicated residue groups, not exponentiation of arbitrary
unmarked lifts. Identifying each group by its retained power of $3$ proves
all assertions and gives the inverse by exponent division. In particular,
\[
 \boxed{3^{da}11^{db}\equiv1\pmod{2^k}
 \iff 3^a11^b\equiv1\pmod{2^h}.}                          \tag{D2}
\]
Equivalently, $J_k\bmod2^{h-2}=J_h$. The lattice of relative exponent relations
is exactly $\mathcal L_h$. Applying the imported theorem separately to
$3^d,11^d$ would inflate both heights; (D2) avoids that loss by an explicit
isomorphism, not by deleting a factor.

Decompose the original $3,11$ rectangle by Euclidean division:
\[
 i=r+dI,\quad j=s+dJ,\quad0\le r,s<d,\qquad
 0\le I\le\left\lfloor\frac{e-r}{d}\right\rfloor,
 \quad0\le J\le\left\lfloor\frac{f-s}{d}\right\rfloor.    \tag{D3}
\]
Only digits $r\le e,s\le f$ occur. For a retained target $t$, first require
$t-r-J_ks\equiv0\pmod d$. The exact higher target is
\[
 I+J_hJ\equiv(t-r-J_ks)/d\pmod{2^{h-2}}.                  \tag{D4}
\]
When the two higher capacities meet (C4) at precision $h$, or their exact
basis widths, (C2) solves (D4) and (D3) returns an actual original word.
The full inverse reads the two lower digits and quotients. Every parameter
occurs in precisely one digit fibre; no inverse-fibre weights are needed here.
Different overlapping affine families must still be kept separate.

This implements the preceding relative boundary, rather than replacing it.
For $o=2^{k-2}$, $d\mid o$ and $o/d\ge2$, put
$\mathcal N_d=\sum_{j=0}^{o/d-1}X^{dj}$ in $\mathbb F_2[C_o]$.
The source map remains
\[
 \iota_d:\mathbb F_2[C_d]\xrightarrow{\sim}\ker((X+1)^d),
 \quad X^r\mapsto X^r\mathcal N_d,
 \qquad \pi_d\iota_d=(o/d)I=0.                            \tag{D5}
\]
Its inverse reads one coefficient per coset after the constant-on-coset image
test. This is the previously proved BR1 instance
\cite[BR1--BR2]{SplitZero}\cite[(B1)--(B4)]{PriorRelative}.
The new capacity theorem constructs available higher words; it does not claim
that their entire counting distribution equals the uniform norm element.
Integer counts and labels remain before parity reduction. A coefficient
reducing to zero stays a supported zero $e=0_A^\bullet$, not absence $\tau$.
No Gamma/Toda metric or analytic source estimate is imported into (D2)--(D4).

\section{Executable precision and original-state examples}
The integer-only logarithm routine uses
\[
 J_k=1+2z\pmod{2^{k-2}},\qquad
 z=\frac{\log(11/3)/8}{\log9/8}\pmod{2^{k-3}}.             \tag{E1}
\]
The denominator is odd. For $c=1,1/3$ the normalized series is
\[
 \frac{\log(1+8c)}8
 =\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{(-1)^{j+1}2^{3(j-1)}c^j}{j}.          \tag{E2}
\]
To compute modulo $2^n$, write $j=2^\nu j_0$, invert only the odd $j_0$, and
retain terms with $3(j-1)-\nu<n$. Summing through $n+2$ is sufficient:
$\nu\le j-1$ makes every later valuation at least $2(j-1)\ge n$.
The first-term valuation and the formal additive law of the convergent
logarithm on $1+8\mathbb Z_2$ prove (E1). Modular exponentiation independently
checks $3^{J_k}=11$, and the known group order proves uniqueness.
There is no floating-point approximation to a discrete logarithm.

At the inherited hard prime $p=482187176641$, $k=6$,
$N=3^7 11^5 37^2$. An exact basis is
\[
 b_1=(2,2),\quad b_2=(-5,3),\quad\det(b_1,b_2)=16,
 \quad(W_3,W_{11})=(7,5).
\]
For the actual outside divisor $37$, the target is $13$. The rounding rule
returns $(i,j)=(1,4)$ and hence
\[
 Q=1625151,\ R=296703,\ a=120546868336,
 \quad(h,r,s,\lambda)=(2,8,7534179271,25393),
\]
\[
 (x,y,z)=(120546868336,184499678869490521595996846,195906863623118608).
\]
The complete original target fibre is $(1,4),(6,1)$; the other point returns
the prior ordered witness. This replays a known occupied branch and is not
new prime coverage. Its primality is checked by full trial division.

At $k=8,v=2$, use the integer input
$N=3^{28}11^{20}31^2$, $p=N-8192$. The lower digits $(0,0)$ reach the
correct quotient class. Precision $h=6$ uses the same $7$ by $5$ high rectangle.
The returned high word is $(5,3)$, hence the original word is $(20,12)$ and
$Q=3^{20}11^{12}31$. The full denominators and inverse are certified.
Primality of this $p$ is not asserted. Here the exact precision map is used;
the asymptotic threshold is not invoked at precision six.

For a required negative comparison, the complete branch
$p=49681$, $k=6$, $N=3^3 11\,13^2$ has no cofactor $-1\pmod{64}$.
Its actual powers do not meet the computed widths. The prime nevertheless has
$(x,y,z)=(12423,56450112,9255769024)$ in another channel/shell. This is an
empty fixed-seed branch, not an ES counterexample.

\paragraph{Finite scope and proof boundary.}
The new checker verifies all $2046$ targets at precisions $3\le k\le12$,
complete corresponding rectangle fibres, signed and axis cases, actual lower
and higher digit inverses, and $258$ exact basis certificates through
$k=4096$. An independent implementation uses direct modular digit lifting
and determinant conditions, not (E1), and checks the exact original divisors.
The main checker rejects seven deliberately false transformations. Executed
counts and hashes are in the generated receipts. No finite experiment proves
Chim's theorem or computes a modulus at either giant onset in (C5).

The remaining global obligation is unchanged in kind: force the actual
factorization of at least one seed of every required prime to provide an
occupied lower target and adequate high capacities, or another original
selector. This work improves the quantitative implication from that inventory;
it does not hypothesize that the inventory is present and call it universal ES.
