\begin{abstract}
A review of the recent programme selects actual bounded prime-factor products
as the next forcing input. In a cyclic $2$-group algebra, an exact dyadic
capacity test places such a product in the nonzero top socle. Binary submask
exponents then give odd positive coefficients at every residue, with original
multiplicities and a terminating ES cofactor return. A formal phase item is
retained as an orientation, not an extra prime. A hard-prime example lies
beyond both the preceding two-block capacity theorem and prime/two-prime
words in either role. Explicit stabilizer constraints complement the test.
The finite comparison includes the stronger simple baseline; no universal
ES theorem or historical-priority claim is made.
\end{abstract}
