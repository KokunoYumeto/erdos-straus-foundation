This note verifies SE1--12 of
\texttt{SIGNED\_STATE\_EVALUATION\_BODY.tex}, using the exact
inverse GF7 in \texttt{GLOBAL\_FIBRE\_ACCEPTED\_BODY.tex}.
The latter is the explicit inverse calculation for the original
four-dimensional polynomial in the canonical ES reader \cite{ES}.
The eight states are retained separately, with both signs at each
of the four literal arithmetic roots. The arithmetic conductor
and its original Gamma metrics are those of WCF \cite{WCF};
the calculation below changes none of their coefficients.

\section{Exact sign decomposition}
Write $h(r)=Ar^4+r^3+Br^2+Cr+D$, where $A\ne0$, and let
$r_j$ be its four distinct roots. Put $d_j=h'(r_j)$ and choose
$a_j$ with $a_j^2d_j=1$, retaining both $a_j$ and $-a_j$.
GF7 gives the complete corresponding point
\[
 q(r,a)=\left(a,-r-i/a,A/a^3+2r/a+3i/a^2,
 7ir^2/a+(B-17r+Ar^2)/a^2-13i/a^3-2A/a^4\right)^T.
 \tag{SR1}
\]
In particular $a^{-1}=ad_j$, $a^{-2}=d_j$, $a^{-3}=ad_j^2$
and $a^{-4}=d_j^2$. Adding and subtracting SR1 at $a_j$ and
$-a_j$ proves
\[
 e_j=\begin{pmatrix}0\\-r_j\\3id_j\\
 (B-17r_j+Ar_j^2)d_j-2Ad_j^2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 o_j=a_j\begin{pmatrix}1\\-id_j\\Ad_j^2+2r_jd_j\\
 7ir_j^2d_j-13id_j^2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 q_{j,\pm}=e_j\pm o_j.
 \tag{SR2}
\]
Let $\mathcal F$ have basis $f_{j,\pm}$ indexed by all eight
points, and define
\[
 \iota_+c_j=(f_{j,+}+f_{j,-})/2,\qquad
 \iota_-c_j=(f_{j,+}-f_{j,-})/2,
\]
where $c_j$ are the four coordinate basis vectors. For any pair
of coefficients $b_{j,+},b_{j,-}$, the decomposition parameters
are $\alpha_j=b_{j,+}+b_{j,-}$ and
$\beta_j=b_{j,+}-b_{j,-}$; hence this is a direct decomposition
$\mathcal F=\operatorname{im}\iota_+\oplus\operatorname{im}\iota_-$.
Set $E=(e_1\ \cdots\ e_4)$, $O=(o_1\ \cdots\ o_4)$ and
$\mathcal Ef_{j,\pm}=q_{j,\pm}$. Formula SR2 proves exactly
\[
 \mathcal E(\iota_+\alpha+\iota_-\beta)=E\alpha+O\beta.
 \tag{SR3}
\]
Thus the factors $1/2$ are present in the map, not removed in a
metric convention.

\section{Independent quartic-algebra determinant calculation}
The following calculation works in the four-dimensional quotient
algebra $\C[r]/(h)$, retaining its original leading coefficient.
In the ordered coefficient basis $(1,r,r^2,r^3)$, multiplication
by $r$ and by $h'(r)$ has matrices
\[
 R=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&-D/A\\1&0&0&-C/A\\
 0&1&0&-B/A\\0&0&1&-1/A\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 F=4AR^3+3R^2+2BR+CI.
 \tag{SR4}
\]
These formulas follow immediately from
$Ar^4=-r^3-Br^2-Cr-D$ in that quotient. If $c_1=(1,0,0,0)^T$,
the transposed coefficient matrix of the four odd polynomials
in SR2 is
\[
 V^{\mathsf T}=\bigl(c_1,\,-iFc_1,\,(AF^2+2RF)c_1,
                 (7iR^2F-13iF^2)c_1\bigr).
 \tag{SR5}
\]
Expanding SR4--5 gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
V_{1,*}&=(1,0,0,0),\\
V_{2,*}&=(-iC,-2iB,-3i,-4iA),\\
V_{3,*}&=(AC^2-9D,4ABC-8AD-7C,
 -16A^2D+4AB^2-2AC-5B,-8A^2C+4AB-3),\\
V_{4,*}&=(-13iC^2+20iD/A,-52iBC+76iD+20iC/A,
 208iAD-52iB^2+5iC+20iB/A,104iAC-66iB+20i/A).
\end{aligned}\tag{SR6}
\]
Expansion along the first row and then the resulting three-by-three
determinant yields the complete rational expression
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \det V=-\frac4A\big(&448A^4D^2-224A^3B^2D+168A^3BC^2
 +856A^3CD+28A^2B^4-22A^2B^2C\\
 &-204A^2BD-431A^2C^2-87AB^3+282ABC+51AD+20B^2-60C\big).
\end{aligned}\tag{SR7}
\]
For the original arithmetic quartet, without changing any root,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 r_{\varepsilon,\eta}&=\tfrac12+\varepsilon\delta+i\eta\gamma,
 &0<\delta<\tfrac12,\quad\gamma>2,\\
 A&=-\tfrac12,&B&=\delta^2-\gamma^2-\tfrac34,\\
 C&=\gamma^2-\delta^2+\tfrac14,
 &D&=-\tfrac1{32}-\tfrac{\gamma^2-\delta^2}{4}
                      -\tfrac{(\delta^2+\gamma^2)^2}{2}.
\end{aligned}\tag{SR8}
\]
Substitution into SR7 and collection of powers gives
\[
 \det V=448\delta^2\gamma^2
             (2\delta^2\gamma^2-\delta^2+\gamma^2)>0.
 \tag{SR9}
\]
The factor in parentheses is
$\gamma^2+\delta^2(2\gamma^2-1)>0$ under the original bounds.
The certificate \texttt{verify\_signed\_evaluation.py}
independently constructs SR4--5, verifies every entry of SR6,
expands SR7, and verifies SR9 as an exact polynomial identity.
It imports no matrices or code from the root calculation.

Let $W=(r_j^n)_{0\le n\le3,1\le j\le4}$ and $\Delta=\det W$.
Evaluation of remainders at roots gives
$O=VW\diag(a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4)$. Also
\[
 \prod_jd_j=A^4\Delta^2,\qquad
 \chi=A^2\Delta\prod_ja_j\in\{1,-1\}.
 \tag{SR10}
\]
Indeed $d_j=A\prod_{k\ne j}(r_j-r_k)$, and the six pair
reversals have total sign $(-1)^6=1$. Squaring $\chi$ using
$a_j^2=d_j^{-1}$ proves its stated values. Consequently
\[
 \det O=\frac{\chi}{A^2}\det V
 =1792\chi\delta^2\gamma^2
       (2\delta^2\gamma^2-\delta^2+\gamma^2)\ne0.
 \tag{SR11}
\]
This keeps the phase of every $a_j$ through $\chi$; only its
modulus will cancel in the later Gram determinant.

\section{Complete rank calculation for the means}
The same quotient-algebra method gives the coefficient matrix
$E_{\mathrm{pol}}$ of the four mean polynomials:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 (E_{\mathrm{pol}})_{1,*}&=(0,0,0,0),\\
 (E_{\mathrm{pol}})_{2,*}&=(0,-1,0,0),\\
 (E_{\mathrm{pol}})_{3,*}&=(3iC,6iB,9i,12iA),\\
 (E_{\mathrm{pol}})_{4,*}&=(w_0,w_1,w_2,w_3),\\
 w_0&=-2AC^2+BC+71D,\\
 w_1&=-8ABC+12AD+2B^2+54C,\\
 w_2&=32A^2D-8AB^2+AC+40B,\\
 w_3&=16A^2C-6AB+20.
\end{aligned}\tag{SR12}
\]
Its last three rows and last three columns have determinant
\[
 -3i(3w_3-4Aw_2)
 =6i(64A^3D-16A^2B^2-22A^2C+89AB-30)
 =6i(16\delta^2\gamma^2-35\delta^2+35\gamma^2)\ne0.
 \tag{SR13}
\]
The final real factor is positive because
$16\delta^2\gamma^2+35(\gamma^2-\delta^2)>0$.
The zero first row bounds the rank by three, and SR13 bounds
it below by three. Since $E=E_{\mathrm{pol}}W$ and $W$ is
invertible, $E$ has rank exactly three.

The even kernel can also be specified by a single explicit vector.
Put
\[
 t=(3w_3-4Aw_2,\ 0,\ 4Aw_0-Cw_3,\ Cw_2-3w_0)^T.
 \tag{SR14}
\]
The first coordinate is nonzero by SR13. Substitution into SR12
gives $E_{\mathrm{pol}}t=0$: in the third and fourth rows the
nontrivial equations are the cross-product cancellation of
$(C,3,4A)$ and $(w_0,w_2,w_3)$, and the second coordinate
is zero. Hence
\[
 \ker E=\C W^{-1}t.
 \tag{SR15}
\]
For completely evaluated coordinates one may use
\[
 (W^{-1}t)_j=
 \frac{t_0(C+Br_j+r_j^2+Ar_j^3)+t_2(1+Ar_j)+t_3A}{d_j}.
 \tag{SR16}
\]
Here $t_0,t_2,t_3$ name the entries corresponding to powers
$r^0,r^2,r^3$. To verify SR16, the $j$th row of $W^{-1}$ is
the coefficient row of the Lagrange polynomial
$h(r)/((r-r_j)d_j)$. Polynomial division gives its coefficients
$d_j^{-1}(C+Br_j+r_j^2+Ar_j^3,B+r_j+Ar_j^2,1+Ar_j,A)$,
whose pairing with $t$ is the displayed expression.

\section{The full kernel, section, and all relabeling maps}
Since $O$ is invertible, SR3 gives the entire kernel and an exact
section without dropping any point:
\[
 \ker\mathcal E
 =\{\iota_+\alpha-\iota_-O^{-1}E\alpha:\alpha\in\C^4\},
 \qquad \mathcal E\iota_-O^{-1}=I.
 \tag{SR17}
\]
The graph parameter is injective because the even and odd
subspaces are a direct sum, so this kernel has dimension four.
Its intersection with the even subspace is the line
$\C\iota_+W^{-1}t$, and its intersection with the odd subspace
is zero. Each statement follows by solving the same equation
$E\alpha+O\beta=0$.

There is an exact invariance under every change of names in
these formulas. Let $Q$ be the permutation matrix whose $j$th
column is $c_{\pi(j)}$, and let
$D_\varepsilon=\diag(\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_4)$,
$\varepsilon_j\in\{1,-1\}$. The new root and sign choices are
$r'_j=r_{\pi(j)}$, $a'_j=\varepsilon_j a_{\pi(j)}$.
Identify the renamed eight-state basis with the original basis
by the points it represents. Then directly from SR2,
\[
 E'=EQ,\quad O'=OQD_\varepsilon,\quad
 \iota'_+=\iota_+Q,\quad\iota'_-=\iota_-QD_\varepsilon,
 \quad (O')^{-1}=D_\varepsilon Q^{-1}O^{-1}.
 \tag{SR18}
\]
Thus
$\iota'_-(O')^{-1}=\iota_-O^{-1}$ exactly. Replacing the
graph parameter by $\alpha'=Q^{-1}\alpha$ also identifies
the graph in SR17 with its renamed presentation. Finally
$\Delta'=(\det Q)\Delta$ and
$\chi'=(\det Q)(\prod_j\varepsilon_j)\chi$, which is
precisely the change in $\det O$. This proves invariance of
the section, kernel, and determinant formula while preserving
all individual root and sign labels.

\section{Return through the unchanged original conductor}
Use the original invertible maps $\Phi:\C^4\to U$ and
$\Psi:\C^4\to W$ with $T_A\Phi=\Psi$, and their original
pulled-back positive Gamma forms $G_U$ and $G$. Define
$\mathcal E_U=\Phi\mathcal E$ and
$\mathcal E_W=\Psi\mathcal E$. Substitution proves
\[
 T_A\mathcal E_U=\mathcal E_W,\qquad
 \ker\mathcal E_U=\ker\mathcal E_W=\ker\mathcal E,
 \qquad
 \mathcal E_W\iota_-O^{-1}\Psi^{-1}=I_W.
 \tag{SR19}
\]
Thus the same invariant section lands in the actual odd
four-plane of the eight-state space. The four target vectors
$\Psi o_j$ have Gram $O^*GO$, and the source vectors $\Phi o_j$
have Gram $O^*G_UO$. Determinant multiplicativity and SR11 give
\[
 \det(O^*GO)=1792^2\delta^4\gamma^4
 (2\delta^2\gamma^2-\delta^2+\gamma^2)^2\det G>0,
 \qquad
 \frac{\det(O^*GO)}{\det(O^*G_UO)}
 =\frac{\det G}{\det G_U}=|\det A_{3,s}|^2.
 \tag{SR20}
\]
The last equality is the already established exact determinant
of the original conductor restriction in its WCF orthonormal
frames. This calculation multiplies the full original forms
by the actual $O$; no entry or off-diagonal term is discarded.

\section{Verification of the complete eight-dimensional return}
For the continuation SE13--17, retain $X,Y$ with $T_AX=Y$ and
the same $\Phi,\Psi$ as in SR19. The two maps
\[
 \mathcal J_U(\iota_+\alpha+\iota_-\beta)
 =(X\alpha,\Phi(E\alpha+O\beta)),\qquad
 \mathcal J_W(\iota_+\alpha+\iota_-\beta)
 =(Y\alpha,\Psi(E\alpha+O\beta))
 \tag{SR21}
\]
are invertible: their entire target inverse is
$\iota_+Y^{-1}p_1+\iota_-O^{-1}(\Psi^{-1}p_2-EY^{-1}p_1)$.
Substitution cancels both terms involving $E$ and gives $p_2$
in the second component; the first component gives $p_1$.
In the other order the same substitution recovers $\alpha,\beta$.
The source proof replaces $Y,\Psi$ with $X,\Phi$. Applying $T_A$
to each component proves $(T_A|_U\oplus T_A|_U)\mathcal J_U
=\mathcal J_W$ exactly.

For the fixed-fibre action
$g f_{j,t}=f_{\pi(j),\sigma_jt}$, put
$\Pi c_j=c_{\pi(j)}$ and $R c_j=\sigma_jc_{\pi(j)}$.
Then $g$ sends $(\alpha,\beta)$ to $(\Pi\alpha,R\beta)$.
Writing $z=E\alpha+O\beta$ gives the full conjugated matrix
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}\Pi&0\\E\Pi-ORO^{-1}E&ORO^{-1}\end{pmatrix}
 \tag{SR22}
\]
in the coordinates $(Y\alpha,\Psi z)$, and the identical
matrix in source coordinates $(X\alpha,\Phi z)$. This is
conjugation by the invertible triangular coordinate map
$(\alpha,\beta)\mapsto(\alpha,E\alpha+O\beta)$, so it
automatically preserves multiplication of all signed
permutations. The actual monodromy subgroup may be restricted
in this exact representation without changing any matrix.

There is a stronger nonvanishing statement for every even sign
element. If $\Pi=I$ and $R$ is a nonidentity diagonal sign
matrix with an even number of minus signs, then
$E-ORO^{-1}E\ne0$. Indeed equality would imply that the
three-dimensional range of $O^{-1}E$ lies in the fixed space
of $R$. That fixed space has dimension two when exactly two
signs are negative and dimension zero when all four are
negative, which is impossible. Thus each such element, when
present in the actual monodromy subgroup, has a nonzero
mixing block; this is stronger than nonvanishing for at least
one element. The formula does not assert invariance of
$\ker\mathcal E$ under a fixed-fibre action. Naming invariance
in SR18 and this action on distinct basis points are different
maps, both specified explicitly above.

Finally set $L=T_A|_U$. The original orthogonal direct sums
have coordinate Grams $\diag(G_x,G_U)$ and $\diag(G_y,G)$.
The exterior space of an orthogonal direct sum decomposes
orthogonally by the number $p$ of vectors from its first
copy and $q$ from its second copy, $p+q=r$. The corresponding
restriction of $\wedge^r(L\oplus L)^{-1}$ is
$(\wedge^pL^{-1})\otimes(\wedge^qL^{-1})$.
Choosing singular bases for these finite-dimensional maps
proves that this tensor product has norm equal to the product
of the two norms. Taking the maximum over the orthogonal
summands therefore proves
\[
 \|\wedge^r(L\oplus L)^{-1}\|
 =\max_{\substack{p+q=r\\0\le p,q\le4}}
       \|\wedge^pL^{-1}\|\,\|\wedge^qL^{-1}\|,
 \qquad 0\le r\le8.
 \tag{SR23}
\]
All maps and metrics here are the original ones on each copy;
no Hilbert norm previously assigned to the eight abstract
basis states is replaced or presumed.
