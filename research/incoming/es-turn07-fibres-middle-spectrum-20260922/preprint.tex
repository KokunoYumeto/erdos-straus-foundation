\begin{abstract}
We complete a source fibre left unclassified by the corrected modulo-24
Erd\H{o}s--Straus trace-return map, and prove that three established inverse
bounds for its literal receiver are exponent-sharp already on actual middle
witnesses with prime $p\equiv1\pmod{840}$. Neither result supplies an initial
witness at every prime. Complete higher-order coefficients, a scalar
observation hierarchy, and independent finite checks are in the accompanying
workbench proof.
\end{abstract}
\maketitle

\section{The bare sorted fibre}
Fix a prime $p\equiv1\pmod{24}$. The original mixed rational source has
$a_0=cq$, $c\mid6$, $q>3$ prime, $p/4<a_0<p/2$, and positive rational tails
whose reciprocal sum with $1/a_0$ is $4/p$ and whose sum is an integer.
With $R_0=4a_0-p$, every such pair has an original word $u_0\mid a_0^2$,
normalized as $a_0=h_0r_0s_0$, $u_0=h_0r_0^2$, $(r_0,s_0)=1$.
Its E tails are $(h_0s_0\kappa,p h_0r_0\kappa)$,
$\kappa=(pr_0+s_0)/R_0$, with a separate swap bit; its M tails are
$(p h_0s_0\lambda,p h_0r_0\lambda)$, $\lambda=(r_0+s_0)/R_0$.
The squared gates are respectively $R_0\mid(4u_0+1)^2$ and
$R_0\mid(p+4u_0)^2$. The factorization
$(R_0y-pa_0)(R_0z-pa_0)=(pa_0)^2$ proves completeness, including both orders.
The coordinate antecedents are Type I/II \citep{ET}.

The corrected source-changing map \citep{Mixed,Composite} writes
$R_0=\delta t^2$ with $\delta$ its squarefree part. E inputs have
$r_0s_0\mid6$ and return the middle state
\[
 H=(\delta+1)/(4r_0s_0),\quad J=(pr_0+s_0)/\delta,
 \qquad (H,s_0,J,r_0).
\]
M inputs are complemented if necessary to a word $\nu\mid c^2\mid36$;
they return that word at $a=(p+\delta)/4$. Sorting forgets the orientations.
This map is denoted by $\rho$.

Let an existing sorted M target be
\[
 W=(p;a,Y,Z)=(p;hrs,phr\lambda,phs\lambda),\qquad r<s,
 \quad R=4a-p=(r+s)/\lambda.
\]
These primitive coordinates are unique: reduce $(Y/p,Z/p)$ by its gcd to
get $(r,s)$, then $h=a/(rs)$ and $\lambda=\gcd(Y/p,Z/p)/h$.
Write $\operatorname{Car}(A)$ for the literal, uniquely decoded condition
$A=cq$, $c\mid6$, $q>3$ prime.
For $n=\prod_\ell\ell^{e_\ell}$ put
$K(n)=\prod_\ell\ell^{\lceil e_\ell/2\rceil}$.

\begin{theorem}
The complete ordered fibre $\rho^{-1}(W)$ is as follows.
Put $\delta_E=4hr\lambda-1$. If $r\lambda\mid6$ and $\delta_E$ is squarefree,
then for every
\[
 t\mid\operatorname{oddpart}(s),\quad\delta_Et^2<p,\quad
 a_t=(p+\delta_Et^2)/4\text{ satisfying }\operatorname{Car},
\]
there are two E inputs, with both orders, and
\[
 (h_t,r_t,s_t)=\left(a_t/(r\lambda),\lambda,r\right),\quad
 u_t=h_t\lambda^2.
\]
There are no other E inputs. For every
$\nu\in\{hr^2,hs^2\}\cap\operatorname{Div}(36)$, if $R$ is squarefree,
put $A_\nu=(p+4\nu)/R$. Every
\[
 t\mid A_\nu,\quad Rt^2<p,\quad
 a_t=(p+Rt^2)/4=c_tq_t,\quad c_t\mid6,\ q_t>3\text{ prime},\quad
 \nu\mid c_t^2
\]
gives exactly the two M words $\nu,a_t^2/\nu$. There are no other M inputs.
All lists are disjoint; hence their cardinalities add with the displayed
factor two.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For E, the normalized target forces
$(r_0,s_0,H,J,\delta)=(\lambda,r,h,s,\delta_E)$.
The reconstructed coordinates satisfy
\[
 r(4u_t+1)=\delta_E(s+\lambda t^2),\qquad
 \gcd(r,\delta_Et^2)=1.
\]
Thus the squared gate is equivalent to
$\delta_Et^2\mid(\delta_Es)^2$, and this is equivalent primewise to
$t\mid\operatorname{oddpart}(s)$ because $\delta_E$ is squarefree and the
residual is odd. Since $\lambda R=r+s$ and $(r,s)=1$, one has
$(s,r\lambda)=1$. Hence $t\mid\operatorname{oddpart}(s)$ gives
$(t,4r\lambda)=1$. Every such unit has square one modulo the divisor
$4r\lambda$ of $24$, so multiplication by $t^2$ preserves the source shell
condition. The displayed original word is therefore available and its forward
return is $W$. The carrier test is exactly the mixed-source restriction.

For M, the sorted target has only the two word orientations $hr^2,hs^2$.
For these two choices respectively,
\[
 A_{hr^2}=4hr\lambda-1,\qquad A_{hs^2}=4hs\lambda-1,
\]
so $A_\nu=(p+4\nu)/R$ is a positive odd integer. The selected small word is
one of them and divides $c_t^2$. Its old squared
gate is $Rt^2\mid(RA_\nu)^2$, exactly $t\mid A_\nu$ when $R$ is squarefree.
Moreover $A_\nu\equiv-1\pmod{4K(\nu)}$, and complementation from $u_t$ to
$u_t'=a_t^2/u_t$ preserves the square gate because
\[
 p+4u_t'\equiv a_tu_t^{-1}(p+4u_t)\pmod{Rt^2}
\]
with a unit multiplier.  Indeed, $\gcd(a_t,Rt^2)=1$ because a common
divisor would divide $4a_t-Rt^2=p$, and $\gcd(u_t,Rt^2)=1$ follows from
$u_t\mid a_t^2$. The original square budget and the gate are therefore
retained. The two
source words have carrier exponent zero and two, hence cannot coincide.
The carrier is unique, so different small words cannot duplicate a source.
E and M inputs have different $p$-valuation pairs in the original factor
coordinates. These same equations give the complete inverse in every case.
\end{proof}

The E general-ray section $t=1$ does not always survive the carrier
restriction. The entire fibre over $(1009;255,24216,686120)$ consists of the
two M words $9,11449$ at $a_t=321=3\cdot107,t=5$. Its squarefree $a=255$
is not a mixed carrier. At $(6841;1866,20523,12765306)$, two E orders from
$a_t=u_t=1713$ and two M words $3,1160652$ from $a_t=1866$ collide.
The free-module kernel over a nonempty undecorated fibre has its literal
difference basis $e_T-e_{T_0}$. After adding the sixteen receiver frames, its
rank is multiplied by sixteen. Neither statement proves a fibre nonempty.

\section{Middle families in the hard class}
The original literal receiver is the typed map
$O:V_{\mathrm{labels}}\to E_{\mathrm{recv}}$ with
\[
 H(X)=-S^{-1}\prod_{j=0}^3(X-\ell_j),\quad
 \ell=(p,a,Y,Z),\quad S=\sum_j\ell_j,\quad A=-1/S,
\]
\[
 d_j=H'(\ell_j),\quad\xi_j^2d_j=1,\qquad
 O_{\cdot j}=\xi_j(1,-id_j,Ad_j^2+2\ell_jd_j,
 i(7\ell_j^2d_j-13d_j^2))^{\mathsf T}.
\]
The established uniform bounds on existing integral witnesses are
$O(p^{3/2}),O(1),O(p^{-2})$ for the first three inverse compounds
\citep[Theorem 1.1]{Sharp}. Norms are the original standard Hermitian norms.

\begin{theorem}
All three uniform powers are optimal already on middle witnesses at primes
$p\equiv1\pmod{840}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First fix $R=11,u=9$, and take primes $p\equiv8401\pmod{9240}$.
Put
\[
 a=(p+R)/4,\qquad Y=p(a+u)/R,\qquad Z=pa(a+u)/(Ru).
\]
Here $9\mid a^2$ and $11\mid p+36$, so these are original integer M states.
More generally fix positive $R,u$, and let
$\alpha=1/(4R)$, $\beta=1/(16Ru)$ and $C=\sqrt{3/(16R)}$.
Choose positive real $\xi_a,\xi_Y$ and positive imaginary $\xi_p,\xi_Z$.
The exact rational roots give
\[
 d_p\sim-C^2p^3,\quad d_a\sim C^2p^3,\quad
 d_Y\sim\alpha^2p^4,\quad d_Z\sim-\beta^2p^6.
\]
The compensated analytic matrix
\[
 B=\operatorname{diag}(1,p^{-3},p^{-4},p^{-6})O
 \operatorname{diag}(p^{3/2},p^{3/2},1,p^{-3})
\]
has limit
\[
 B_0=\begin{pmatrix}
i/C&1/C&0&0\\-C&-iC&0&0\\
-2iC&C/2&2\alpha^2&0\\
13C^3&-13iC^3&-6i\alpha^3&20\beta^3
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The four leading compound norms are
\[
20\beta^3p^9,\quad40\alpha^2\beta^3p^{13},\quad
40\sqrt2C\alpha^2\beta^3p^{29/2},\quad80\alpha^2\beta^3p^{13}.
\]
They use respectively the entry $(3,Z)$, the minor $(2,3;Y,Z)$, both
triples $(1,2,3;p,Y,Z)$ and $(1,2,3;a,Y,Z)$, and the full determinant.
Every other compound entry is a lower power, as follows by adding row
powers $(0,3,4,6)$ and column powers $(-3/2,-3/2,0,3)$ and retaining zero
leading minors. The complete leading row and the Frobenius bound therefore
sandwich the true compound norms. Consequently
\[
 p^{-3/2}\|O^{-1}\|\to\sqrt{3/(32R)},\qquad
 \|\wedge^2O^{-1}\|\to\tfrac12.
\]
These nonzero limits settle the first two powers.

For the third, take primes $p\equiv4201\pmod{9240}$ and set
$s=(p+1)/11$, $(a,Y,Z)=(3s,3p,3ps)$.
The original M coordinates are $(h,r,s,\lambda)=(3,1,s,1)$.
The small root ratios are $x=(1,3/11,3)$, their derivative constants are
$D=(-16/11,240/121,60/11)$, and $Z\sim(3/11)p^2$.
The leading core matrix in rows $0,1,2$ is obtained by multiplying each
column $(1,-iD_j,2x_jD_j)^{\mathsf T}$ by the selected inverse square root
of $D_j$. Its determinant is $2896/121$. The last row of $O$ on the $Z$
column is $20(3/11)^3p^6[1+O(p^{-1})]$; eliminating that column affects
only lower core powers. The full determinant is therefore that entry times
$(2896/121)p^2[1+O(p^{-1})]$. Since
$\|\wedge^3O^{-1}\|=\|O\|/|\det O|$,
\[
 p^2\|\wedge^3O^{-1}\|\longrightarrow121/2896>0.
\]
Both residues are coprime to $9240$ and are $1\pmod{840}$.
Dirichlet's theorem supplies infinitely many prime members
\citep{DirichletOriginal}; see also \citet[Theorem 18.1]{Dirichlet}.
No simultaneous primality of an additional
carrier, nor universal occupancy of the mixed trace source, is asserted.
\end{proof}

The complete proof gives all first singular corrections, not just rates:
\[
6R+14u/5,\quad -R/2+19u/5,\quad-41R/12,\quad11R/12.
\]
It also proves the middle scalar heat-observation hierarchy $0,2,3,9$,
retaining the small last coordinate, original signs, and metric factors.
Those refinements are not needed for the theorem just proved.
