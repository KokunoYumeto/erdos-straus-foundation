## 1. The complete sorted-return fibre

Let

$$
W=(p;a,Y,Z),\qquad a<Y<Z,
$$

be any existing middle witness at \(p\equiv1\pmod{24}\). Recover its unique primitive orientation by

$$
b=Y/p,\qquad c=Z/p,\qquad g=\gcd(b,c),
$$

$$
r=b/g<s=c/g,\qquad h=\frac{a}{rs},\qquad\lambda=\frac gh.
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{
a=hrs,\quad Y=phr\lambda,\quad Z=phs\lambda,\quad
R=4a-p=\frac{r+s}{\lambda}.
}
$$

These are the inherited Type-II coordinates, with the sorted target fixing their orientation (Elsholtz & Tao, 2013, §2, Proposition 2.6). ([arXiv][1])

Write \(\operatorname{Car}(A)\) for the **actual finite test**

$$
A=cq,\qquad c\in\{1,2,3,6\},\qquad q>3\text{ prime}.
$$

When present, the pair \((c,q)\) is unique. This test is executed; it is not an assumed prime-supply condition.

### Exterior-origin inputs

Put

$$
\delta_E=4hr\lambda-1.
$$

There are no E-origin mixed inputs unless

$$
r\lambda\mid6,\qquad \delta_E\text{ is squarefree}.
$$

When those conditions hold, define

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal I_E(W)=
\left\{
t\mid\operatorname{oddpart}(s):
\delta_Et^2<p,\quad
\operatorname{Car}\!\left(\frac{p+\delta_Et^2}{4}\right)
\right\}.
}
$$

For each such \(t\), the complete original source is

$$
R_t=\delta_Et^2,\qquad a_t=\frac{p+R_t}{4},
$$

$$
\boxed{
(h_t,r_t,s_t)
=
\left(\frac{a_t}{r\lambda},\lambda,r\right),
\qquad u_t=h_t\lambda^2.
}
$$

It contributes exactly two ordered E inputs, obtained by retaining the exterior tail-swap bit.

The corrected forward return forces these parameters. Indeed,

$$
p\lambda+r=\delta_Es.
$$

The squared trace gate becomes

$$
\delta_Et^2\mid(\delta_Es)^2.
$$

Because \(\delta_E\) is squarefree, primewise comparison gives exactly

$$
t\mid\operatorname{oddpart}(s).
$$

Unit squares modulo \(4r\lambda\mid24\) preserve the original shell congruence. The carrier test is the remaining restriction from the general automatic-ray source to the mixed source.

### Middle-origin inputs

Set

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal V(W)=\{hr^2,hs^2\}\cap\operatorname{Div}(36).
}
$$

There are no M-origin mixed inputs unless the target residual \(R\) is squarefree.

For each \(\nu\in\mathcal V(W)\), put

$$
A_\nu=\frac{p+4\nu}{R}.
$$

The complete index set is

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal I_{M,\nu}(W)=
\{t\mid A_\nu:\;&Rt^2<p,\quad
a_t=(p+Rt^2)/4=c_tq_t,\\
&c_t\mid6,\quad q_t>3\text{ prime},\quad
\nu\mid c_t^2\}.
\end{aligned}}
$$

All these \(t\) are odd.

Each index gives precisely the two original M words

$$
\boxed{u_t=\nu,\qquad u_t=a_t^2/\nu}
$$

at residual \(R_t=Rt^2\).

To prove completeness, the squarefree target must be the squarefree part of the old residual. Its pre-complement small word is one of the two target divisor orientations, hence \(\nu\in\mathcal V(W)\). The original squared gate is

$$
Rt^2\mid(p+4\nu)^2=(RA_\nu)^2.
$$

Since \(R\) is squarefree, that is exactly \(t\mid A_\nu\).

The carrier and its original exponent budget remain explicit. Distinct \(\nu\) cannot duplicate an M source: the source’s unique carrier prime identifies which of \(u_t,a_t^2/u_t\) is the small word.

### Exact count and inverse

The two origin channels are disjoint in the original rational factor coordinates. Consequently,

$$
\boxed{
|\rho^{-1}(W)|
=
2|\mathcal I_E(W)|
+
2\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal V(W)}
|\mathcal I_{M,\nu}(W)|.
}
$$

Every item reconstructs

$$
p,a_t,R_t,u_t,h_t,r_t,s_t,\kappa_t\text{ or }\lambda_t
$$

and its original ordered rational tails. Every prime-exponent box is recalculated at the appropriate shell.

This is a complete finite inverse for an arbitrary supplied sorted M target. An empty fibre is an allowed, exactly decided result.

## 2. Sorting loses more than a complement bit

The general automatic-ray section \(t=1\) does not necessarily belong to the mixed carrier domain.

For example,

$$
\boxed{W=(1009;255,24216,686120)}
$$

has exactly two mixed inputs: the M words \(9\) and \(11449\) at

$$
a_t=321=3\cdot107,\qquad R_t=275,\qquad t=5.
$$

Both inputs are proper rational traces. The squarefree representative \(a=255\) is not a permitted mixed carrier, so the fibre has **no \(t=1\) input**.

Similarly,

$$
W=(1129;308,3387,1043196)
$$

has only the two E tail orders at

$$
a_t=417=3\cdot139,\qquad t=7.
$$

Its prospective \(t=1\) carrier would be \(285\), again outside the mixed source.

There are also genuine E/M-origin collisions. At

$$
\boxed{W=(6841;1866,20523,12765306),}
$$

the full fibre consists of the two E orders at

$$
a_t=u_t=1713=3\cdot571
$$

and the two M words

$$
3,\qquad1160652
$$

at \(a_t=1866=6\cdot311\). These are four distinct original inputs of one bare sorted target.

For the free abelian coefficient map onto its actual image, choose an actual source \(T_W\) in each nonempty fibre. Its kernel is exactly

$$
\boxed{
\bigoplus_W
\left\langle e_T-e_{T_W}:T\ne T_W,\ \rho(T)=W\right\rangle,
}
$$

of rank

$$
\sum_W\bigl(|\rho^{-1}(W)|-1\bigr).
$$

The section chooses a member of the computed fibre; it does not insert an unavailable \(t=1\) source.

With original positive coefficient forms \(G_T\), the receiver sum

$$
z=O_W\sum_{\rho(T)=W}v_T
$$

has exact quotient form

$$
\boxed{
Q_W=
\left[
O_W\left(\sum_{\rho(T)=W}G_T^{-1}\right)O_W^*
\right]^{-1}.
}
$$

For equal Euclidean forms, the minimum-norm section is

$$
v_T=|\rho^{-1}(W)|^{-1}O_W^{-1}z.
$$

It generally is not an integral coefficient section. The original integer kernel and the metric minimum remain different constructions.

## 3. Middle states have a different large-parameter spectrum

The integrated sharp theorem already gives, on existing integral witnesses,

$$
\|O^{-1}\|_2=O(p^{3/2}),\qquad
\|\wedge^2O^{-1}\|_2=O(1),\qquad
\|\wedge^3O^{-1}\|_2=O(p^{-2}).
$$

The question addressed here is their sharpness and finer behaviour on **middle witnesses in the hard class**, not another proof of those upper bounds.

Fix integers \(R\equiv3\pmod4\), \(u>0\), with \(\gcd(R,u)=1\). At the prime parameters satisfying

$$
a=\frac{p+R}{4}\in\mathbb Z,\qquad
u\mid a^2,\qquad R\mid p+4u,
$$

retain the sorted middle family

$$
\boxed{
(a,Y,Z)=
\left(
a,\frac{p(a+u)}R,\frac{pa(a+u)}{Ru}
\right).
}
$$

The formulas define the exact rational family used for expansion; primality and integer availability are imposed separately when asserting original witnesses.

Put

$$
\alpha=\frac1{4R},\qquad
\beta=\frac1{16Ru},\qquad
C=\sqrt{\frac3{16R}}.
$$

For \(p>4(R+u)\), the literal order is

$$
a<p<Y<Z.
$$

Choose positive real square roots for \(d_a,d_Y>0\), and positive-imaginary inverse roots for \(d_p,d_Z<0\). The oriented determinant sign is then fixed.

## 4. The middle observation hierarchy is \(0,2,3,9\)

Let

$$
v_0=\frac{(-i,1,0,0)^{\mathsf T}}{\sqrt2},
\qquad
w_0=\frac{(i,1,0,0)^{\mathsf T}}{\sqrt2},
\qquad
n_0=\frac C{\sqrt2}.
$$

The normalized right singular vector for \(s_4\), with positive \(a\)-component, has

$$
v_p
=
v_0-\frac{9R}{4p}w_0
+\frac{9R}{8n_0}p^{-3/2}e_Y
+O_{R,u}(p^{-2}).
$$

The two small coordinates have stronger **relative** expansions:

$$
\boxed{
e_Y^*v_p=
\frac{9R}{8n_0}p^{-3/2}
\left[
1+\frac{7R/4-38u/5}{p}+O_{R,u}(p^{-2})
\right],
}
$$

$$
\boxed{

\frac{576iRu^3}{5n_0}p^{-9/2}
\left[
1+\frac{-35R/4-239u/40}{p}+O_{R,u}(p^{-2})
\right].
}
$$

The last component is proved through the exact inverse factorization; it is not inferred from the much larger error in the full vector.

Also,

$$
\boxed{
p^3s_4^2=\frac{32R}{3}+\frac{176R^2}{9p}
+O_{R,u}(p^{-2}).
}
$$

For a fixed nonzero scalar row \(L\) and fixed \(\tau>0\), consider

$$
L\exp(-\tau p^3O^*O)L^*.
$$

Its complete hierarchy is:

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{first nonzero original observation}&
\text{rescaling power}&
\text{limiting coefficient}\\ \hline

\end{array}
$$

Every coefficient is multiplied by

$$
e^{-(32R/3)\tau}.
$$

The four tests are exhaustive because \(v_0,w_0,e_Y,e_Z\) span the original label space. There is no assertion at \(\tau=0\).

The integrated exterior fixed-\(c\) family has last observation power \(15\). That result remains correct on its own source. The middle family’s last inverse-column scale is \(p^{-3}\), rather than \(p^{-6}\), and its corresponding normalized observation power is \(9\).

## 5. All three inverse exponents are sharp on hard middle witnesses

The preceding family has actual prime members. Take

$$
\boxed{R=11,\qquad u=9,\qquad p\equiv8401\pmod{9240}.}
$$

The progression is reduced, lies in \(1\bmod840\), and supplies

$$
11\mid p+36,\qquad
3\mid a=(p+11)/4.
$$

Hence \(9\mid a^2\), and every prime in the progression gives the original M witness above. Dirichlet’s theorem applies after these reducedness and availability checks (Sutherland, 2017, Theorem 18.1). ([MIT Mathematics][2])

Thus, already on middle witnesses in the hard class,

$$
p^{-3/2}\|O^{-1}\|_2
\longrightarrow\sqrt{\frac3{352}}>0,
\qquad
\|\wedge^2O^{-1}\|_2\longrightarrow\frac12.
$$

The powers \(p^{3/2}\) and \(p^0\) cannot be improved uniformly there.

For the third compound, use the different reduced progression

$$
\boxed{p\equiv4201\pmod{9240},\qquad s=\frac{p+1}{11}.}
$$

It gives the original middle state

$$
(h,r,s,\lambda)=(3,1,s,1),
$$

with sorted denominators

$$
\boxed{(a,Y,Z)=(3s,3p,3ps).}
$$

The initial prime \(4201\) gives

$$
(1146,12603,4814346).
$$

The three small literal root ratios tend to

$$
1,\qquad 3/11,\qquad3.
$$

Their leading receiver coefficient matrix is

$$

\begin{pmatrix}

-4\sqrt{11}/11&-4i\sqrt{15}/11&-2i\sqrt{165}/11\\

The complete compound calculation gives

$$

$$

The last limit proves sharpness of the inherited \(p^{-2}\) exponent on hard middle witnesses.

The current repository already contains the exterior \(c=29\) family in the hard progression \(3361\bmod24360\). I have not re-presented that family as new. The present extension establishes the three exponents on the **middle** domain, with different original states and constants.
