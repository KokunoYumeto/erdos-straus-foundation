## Turn 7 continuation: the fixed-target capacity problem is now completely classified

The preceding negative-square transfer required an available divisor of \(((h+1)/4)^2\). That requirement has now been resolved as an exact arithmetic problem, rather than weakened by an assumption.

The main result is:

$$
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
h=4j-1>t,\qquad t\nmid j^2,\\
\exists W\mid j^2,\quad W\equiv t\pmod h
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
h\le t^2-3t+1.
\end{gathered}}
$$

Every exceptional word below that bound has an explicit, uniquely invertible parametrization. The bound is attained by **original exterior states at infinitely many hard primes**, for unbounded values of \(t\).

There is also a definitive limitation: **for every fixed \(t\ge4\), infinitely many hard primes have an original exterior state with \(\alpha=-4t\) but an empty complete middle cofactor box at its original grade \(h\)**. Those primes already have the constructed exterior solution. This refutes universal repair at that grade, not ES.

On the positive side, the original equations force the necessary divisor budget on three entire \(\alpha\)-lines and nine entire \(\beta\)-lines, including nonsquare coefficients. They give actual same-prime middle returns.

This completes an unbounded subproblem within Turn 7. It does not complete the universal existence milestone.

## 1. The original source and the exact capacity being tested

Retain a first-half exterior state at a prime \(p\equiv1\pmod4\):

$$
\frac p4<a<\frac p2,\qquad
R=4a-p,\qquad
u\mid a^2,\qquad
R\mid4u+1.
$$

Its original normalization is

$$
g=\gcd(a,u),\qquad
(h,r,s)=\left(\frac{g^2}{u},\frac ug,\frac ag\right),
\qquad
\kappa=\frac{pr+s}{R}.
$$

Thus

$$
a=hrs,\qquad u=hr^2,\qquad \gcd(r,s)=1,
$$

and the ordered return is

$$
\boxed{
\frac4p=\frac1a+\frac1{hs\kappa}+\frac1{phr\kappa}.
}
$$

The ordered inverse remains

$$
u=\frac{a^2}{Ry-pa}.
$$

These are the inherited Type-I/II coordinates, not a new parametrization claim (Elsholtz & Tao, 2013, §2, Propositions 2.2 and 2.6).

Keep

$$
v=hs^2,\qquad
D=\frac{4u+1}{R},\qquad
\alpha=v-R,\qquad
\beta=v-D.
$$

The original equations imply

$$
h\mid\alpha\beta-1,\qquad
p\equiv\alpha\pmod h,\qquad
p\beta\equiv1\pmod h.
$$

Suppose either marked shape coordinate is

$$
-4t,\qquad t\in\mathbb Z_{>0}.
$$

The coefficient \(t\) need not be a square. Then

$$
\boxed{
h\equiv3\pmod4,\qquad
s\text{ is odd},\qquad
\gcd(h,t)=1,\qquad
\left(\frac th\right)=1.
}
$$

Indeed, \(hs^2\equiv3\pmod4\); the grade congruences supply coprimality. Reciprocity on the original divisor source gives \((h/p)=-1\), while either negative-shape congruence gives

$$
\left(\frac hp\right)
=
-\left(\frac th\right).
$$

Put

$$
j=\frac{h+1}{4}.
$$

The relevant **complete original capacity** is

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal A_t(j)=
\{W\in\mathbb Z_{>0}:W\mid j^2,\quad W\equiv t\pmod{4j-1}\}.
}
$$

For every \(W\in\mathcal A_t(j)\), use

$$
U=
\begin{cases}
W,&\alpha=-4t,\\
j^2/W,&\beta=-4t.
\end{cases}
$$

Then

$$
Q=h,\qquad
R_M=\frac{p+4U}{h},\qquad
a_M=\frac{p+R_M}{4}
$$

is an original middle source.

The inverse normalization is

$$
\boxed{
g_M=\gcd(j,U),\quad
h_M=\frac{g_M^2}{U},\quad
r_M=\frac U{g_M},\quad
\lambda_M=\frac j{g_M},\quad
s_M=R_M\lambda_M-r_M.
}
$$

It returns

$$
\boxed{
(a_M,\ ph_Ms_M\lambda_M,\ ph_Mr_M\lambda_M).
}
$$

This is the full cofactor-\(h\) gate, not merely a selected sufficient family. For the \(\beta\)-branch,

$$
p\equiv-(4t)^{-1}\pmod h,\qquad
j^2\equiv16^{-1}\pmod h,
$$

so \(U=j^2/W\) satisfies the gate exactly when \(W\equiv t\).

All range conditions follow from the actual source:

$$
p>2h,\qquad
4U\le4j^2=\frac{(h+1)^2}{4}<(h-1)p.
$$

Hence \(0<R_M<p\). The identity

$$
a_M=R_Mj-U=h_Mr_Ms_M
$$

proves positivity, and the prime equation proves \(\gcd(r_M,s_M)=1\). An orientation with \(r_M>s_M\) remains valid and is retained rather than discarded.

The previous theorem handled \(t=c^2\) when \(c\mid j\). The present capacity includes every original divisor, whether or not it is square.

## 2. Every available divisor has one of three exact forms

Let \(t,j\ge1\) and \(h=4j-1>t\). There is no primality assumption on \(h\).

### Complete capacity theorem

The set \(\mathcal A_t(j)\) is the disjoint union of the following words.

The **canonical word** is

$$
\boxed{W=t,}
$$

present exactly when \(t\mid j^2\).

The **companion word** is

$$
\boxed{W=4tj,}
$$

present exactly when \(4t\mid j\).

Every other word has the unique finite parametrization

$$
\boxed{
1\le\ell\le\left\lfloor\frac{t-2}{4}\right\rfloor,\qquad
w\mid\ell^2,\qquad
n=\frac{t-w}{4\ell+1}\in\mathbb Z_{>0},
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
V=\frac{\ell^2}{w},\qquad
j=\ell+4nV,\qquad
h=4j-1>t,\qquad
W=\frac{j^2}{V}=t+nh.
}
$$

The displayed \(j\) must equal the input \(j\).

The complete inverse for \(W\ne t\) is

$$
\boxed{
n=\frac{W-t}{h},\qquad
V=\frac{j^2}{W},\qquad
\ell=j-4nV.
}
$$

If \(\ell=0\), the word is the companion. Otherwise

$$
w=\ell^2/V
$$

recovers its finite label.

### Proof

If \(W\ne t\), positivity and \(0<t<h\) give

$$
W=t+nh,\qquad n\ge1.
$$

Set \(V=j^2/W\). Since

$$
16WV=16j^2=(h+1)^2,
$$

there is a positive integer \(k\) with

$$
16tV=1+kh.
$$

Reduction modulo four gives \(k\equiv1\pmod4\). Write \(k=4\ell+1\), \(\ell\ge0\).

Comparing the exact products gives

$$
h+2=k+16nV,
$$

and therefore

$$
\boxed{
j=\ell+4nV,\qquad
t=(4\ell+1)n+\frac{\ell^2}{V}.
}
$$

If \(\ell=0\), then \(n=t\), \(j=4tV\), and \(W=4tj\).

If \(\ell>0\), the last expression proves \(V\mid\ell^2\). With \(w=\ell^2/V\ge1\),

$$
t\ge4\ell+2,
$$

giving the finite range.

Conversely, every permitted finite record satisfies

$$
\frac{j^2}{V}
=
w+8\ell n+16n^2V
=
t+n(4j-1).
$$

It is therefore an actual divisor in the required class. The inverse formulas distinguish every word and every case.

The separate domain \(h\le t\) has

$$
j\le\left\lfloor\frac{t+1}{4}\right\rfloor
$$

and remains a literal finite divisor calculation. The large-grade theorem is not asserted there.

## 3. The finite-repair bound is sharp

For a finite word,

$$
h+1
=
4\ell+
\frac{16\ell^2(t-w)}{(4\ell+1)w}
\le
4\ell+\frac{16\ell^2(t-1)}{4\ell+1}.
$$

The last expression is strictly increasing in \(\ell\ge0\). Substituting

$$
\ell\le(t-2)/4
$$

gives

$$
\boxed{h\le t^2-3t+1.}
$$

Equality holds exactly when

$$
\boxed{w=n=1,\qquad t=4\ell+2.}
$$

The arithmetic equality family is

$$
\boxed{
t=4\ell+2,\quad
j=\ell(4\ell+1),\quad
h=t^2-3t+1,\quad
W=(4\ell+1)^2,\quad
V=\ell^2.
}
$$

Here \(t\nmid j^2\): the odd factor \(2\ell+1>1\) of \(t\) is coprime to both factors of \(j\).

The companion must be treated separately. It is unbounded, but it cannot repair missing canonical availability because

$$
4t\mid j\Longrightarrow t\mid j^2.
$$

Consequently,

$$
\boxed{
h>\max\{t,t^2-3t+1\}
\Longrightarrow
|\mathcal A_t(j)|
=
\mathbf1_{t\mid j^2}+\mathbf1_{4t\mid j}.
}
$$

A finite exceptional word can coexist with the canonical one. For example,

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal A_{289}(1224)=\{289,5184\}.
}
$$

The second word has

$$
(n,V,\ell,w)=(1,289,68,16).
$$

The original coefficient is two, not one because the support occupies a single residue class.

The finite search depends only on \(t\):

$$
\sum_{\ell\le(t-2)/4}\tau_{\mathrm{div}}(\ell^2)
\le L(1+\log L)^2,\qquad
L=\left\lfloor\frac{t-2}{4}\right\rfloor.
$$

This follows from \(\tau_{\mathrm{div}}(\ell^2)\le d_3(\ell)\) and the elementary harmonic estimate for \(\sum_{abc\le L}1\).

There is also a smaller reconstructed grade. Put

$$
g_0=\gcd(\ell,V),\qquad
H_0=g_0^2/V,\quad r_0=V/g_0,\quad\lambda_0=\ell/g_0.
$$

Then the middle normalization is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\alpha\text{-branch}:&
\quad(h_M,r_M,\lambda_M)
=(H_0,\lambda_0+4nr_0,r_0),\\
\beta\text{-branch}:&
\quad(h_M,r_M,\lambda_M)
=(H_0,r_0,\lambda_0+4nr_0),
\end{aligned}
$$

with

$$
\boxed{h_M\le\ell<t/4.}
$$

This is a proved reduction within the finite capacity calculation. It is not a descent argument that supplies an initial ES state.

## 4. Original arithmetic forces the budget on nonsquare shape lines

At every prime \(p\equiv1\pmod{24}\), an actual exterior source forces canonical availability on

$$
\boxed{
\alpha=-4t,\qquad t\in\{1,2,3\},
}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{
\beta=-4t,\qquad t\mid36.
}
$$

Thus the nine beta coefficients are

$$
1,\ 2,\ 3,\ 4,\ 6,\ 9,\ 12,\ 18,\ 36.
$$

There is no bound on the other shape coordinate.

For the alpha lines, \((t/h)=1\) and \(h\equiv3\pmod4\) suffice. At \(t=2\), they force \(h\equiv7\pmod8\), hence \(2\mid j\). At \(t=3\), they force \(h\equiv11\pmod{12}\), hence \(3\mid j\). The case \(t=1\) is immediate.

The beta proof uses more than that character. The actual equations give

$$
D=hs^2+4t,\qquad
r+\kappa=sD,\qquad
pD+1=4hr\kappa.
$$

Since \(sD\) is odd, \(r\kappa\) is even. Therefore \(D\equiv7\pmod8\), and

$$
h\equiv7-4t\pmod8.
$$

For even \(t\), this proves \(2\mid j\).

When \(3\mid t\), the same equations force \(h\equiv2\pmod3\). Indeed, \(h=0\pmod3\) would give \(1=0\). If \(h=1\), then \(s=0\) would require \(1=-r^2\); if \(s=\pm1\), it would require

$$
2=r(s-r),
$$

whose right side has only values zero and one modulo three. Thus \(3\mid j\).

Every exponent in a divisor of \(36\) is at most two. These forced divisibilities therefore prove

$$
t\mid j^2
$$

on all nine beta lines.

For a canonical word, the squarefree part of the new grade \(h_M\) is exactly that of \(t\). The earlier square-grade return is its \(t=c^2\) special case. Nonsquare \(t\) is not normalized away.

## 5. The sharp bound occurs in original hard-prime states

The equality family is not merely an abstract divisor example.

For every

$$
t=6+420z,\qquad z\ge0,
$$

put

$$
h=t^2-3t+1,\qquad
R=t^2+t+1,\qquad
\ell=(t-2)/4.
$$

Then

$$
\gcd(h,R)=1,\qquad \gcd(hR,210)=1.
$$

Also \(t^3\equiv1\pmod R\), so the integer

$$
r_0=\frac{t+2R}{4}
$$

satisfies

$$
16tr_0^2\equiv1\pmod R.
$$

Choose \(r\) by

$$
r\equiv r_0\pmod R,\qquad
r\equiv\frac{R+1}{4}h^{-1}\pmod{210}.
$$

Then

$$
p=4hr-R
$$

is a reduced arithmetic progression modulo \(840hR\), with \(p\equiv1\pmod{840}\). Modulo \(h\), it equals \(-R\); modulo \(R\), it equals \(4hr_0\). Those are units, proving the reducedness needed for Dirichlet’s theorem.

Hence there are infinitely many prime members (Sutherland, 2017, Theorem 18.1). Each sufficiently large member has the original exterior state

$$
a=hr,\qquad u=hr^2,\qquad s=1,
$$

with

$$
\alpha=h-R=-4t.
$$

Its canonical cofactor word is unavailable, while

$$
W=(t-1)^2
$$

is the sharp finite repair.

This uses primes in a **proved reduced linear progression**. It does not assume infinitely many primes represented by an unproved polynomial family.

### A concrete hard-prime repair

At

$$
\boxed{p=825241\equiv361\pmod{840}},
$$

the original exterior marking is

$$
\boxed{
(a,R,u,h,r,s,\kappa)
=
(206321,43,2240439739,19,10859,1,208402140).
}
$$

Here

$$
\alpha=-24,\qquad t=6,\qquad j=5.
$$

The complete cofactor box is

$$
\{1,5,25\},
$$

and only

$$
25\equiv6\pmod{19}
$$

hits. The unavailable canonical word is \(6\).

The return is

$$
\boxed{
(a_M,R_M,U,h_M,r_M,s_M,\lambda_M)
=
(217170,43439,25,1,5,43434,1),
}
$$

giving

$$
\boxed{
\frac4{825241}
=
\frac1{217170}
+\frac1{825241\cdot43434}
+\frac1{825241\cdot5}.
}
$$

Its original grade satisfies

$$
19=6^2-3\cdot6+1.
$$

Thus the sharp finite-repair boundary is attained by actual original prime arithmetic, and the return genuinely goes beyond the preceding square-coefficient rule.

## 6. For every fixed alpha coefficient \(t\ge4\), universal same-grade repair is false

The finite classification also settles the negative side.

Let

$$
K(t)=\prod_{\ell^e\parallel t}\ell^{\lceil e/2\rceil},
\qquad M=4K(t).
$$

Consider the unit classes

$$
A\equiv3\pmod4,\qquad \left(\frac tA\right)=1.
$$

If \(t\) is square, this set has \(\varphi(M)/2\) elements. If \(t\) is nonsquare, it has \(\varphi(M)/4\). For the latter count, the positive quadratic character of \(t\) is nontrivial on units congruent to one modulo four: choose a nonsquare at an odd prime occurring to odd exponent, or the class five modulo eight when only the two-part is nonsquare. CRT makes the other coordinates one.

The class \(-1\) always belongs to the set. It is the **only** class exactly for

$$
\boxed{t=1,2,3.}
$$

Now fix \(t\ge4\) and choose \(A\ne-1\) in that set. Lift it by CRT so that both \(R\) and \(R-4t\) are units at \(3,5,7\). If one of those primes divides \(t\), the unit condition already suffices; otherwise at most two residue values are excluded.

Dirichlet supplies a prime \(R\) in this class so large that

$$
h=R-4t>\max\{4t,t^2-3t+1\}.
$$

Since \(R\not\equiv-1\pmod{4K(t)}\),

$$
t\nmid j^2,\qquad j=(h+1)/4.
$$

The choice \((t/R)=1\) supplies an actual root

$$
16tr_0^2\equiv1\pmod R.
$$

Solve the same two CRT conditions on \(r\):

$$
r\equiv r_0\pmod R,\qquad
r\equiv\frac{R+1}{4}h^{-1}\pmod{210}.
$$

Again \(p=4hr-R\) is a reduced progression of primes \(1\pmod{840}\). Its original state

$$
(a,u)=(hr,hr^2)
$$

has \(\alpha=-4t\).

But the completed capacity theorem now gives

$$
\boxed{\mathcal A_t(j)=\varnothing.}
$$

The canonical divisor is absent; its companion is therefore absent; and the finite-repair range has been passed.

Consequently:

$$
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
\text{For every fixed }t\ge4,\text{ infinitely many hard primes have}\\
\text{an original E state with }\alpha=-4t
\text{ and no M return at its original cofactor }h.
\end{gathered}}
$$

Every prime constructed in this proof already has its exterior solution. The theorem concerns the complete **same-grade middle box**, not the whole ES source.

For \(t=4\), an explicit progression is

$$
R=59,\qquad h=43,\qquad j=11,
$$

$$
r=8415+12390n,\qquad
\boxed{p=1447321+2131080n.}
$$

At \(n=3\),

$$
p=7840561
$$

is prime and has the original exterior marking

$$
(a,R,u,h,r,s,\kappa)
=
(1960155,59,89353665675,43,45585,1,6057830054).
$$

The complete capacity divisors \(1,11,121\) have residues

$$
1,\ 11,\ 35\pmod{43},
$$

not four.

There are corresponding finite limitations to the beta automatic theorem. The certificate exhausts the old-grade boxes for actual hard-prime beta sources with

$$
\boxed{
(t,p,h)=(8,346201,23),\quad
(27,593401,11),\quad
(5,2727841,11).
}
$$

All three boxes are empty. No infinite beta-obstruction theorem is inferred from those examples.

## 7. The inverse fibres retain the original gate

The complete fixed-\((p,h,t)\) alpha fibre has

$$
N=\frac{p+4t}{h},\qquad
s\mid N,\qquad
r=\frac{s+N/s}{4}\in\mathbb Z,
$$

together with

$$
\boxed{
\gcd(r,s)=1,\qquad
\frac p4<hrs<\frac p2,\qquad
4hrs-p\mid4hr^2+1.
}
$$

The last condition is necessary. The factor equation and shape alone do not imply it.

This repairs one omission in the predecessor’s **prose inverse domain**. At

$$
p=5209,\qquad h=95,\qquad t=4,
$$

the factor equation has \(N=55\). Choosing \(s=5,r=4\) satisfies its divisibility, coprimality, shape and first-half tests, but gives

$$
a=1900,\qquad R=2391,\qquad u=1520,
$$

and

$$
(4u+1)\bmod R=1299.
$$

It is not an exterior state.

The predecessor executable already rejected it through the E-gate check. The correction concerns the textual claim of a complete inverse domain, not the forward transfer theorem. The old source remains preserved.

For beta, the full fibre retains

$$
F=\frac{4tp+1}{h},\qquad
D=hs^2+4t,\qquad
\Delta_s=s^2(D^2-p)-F,
$$

and the positive square condition \(\Delta_s=k^2\), with

$$
r=(sD-k)/2,\qquad \kappa=sD-r,
$$

plus the original range, parity and coprimality checks.

The new map keeps the original E record, side, \(t,j,W\), and canonical/companion/finite label. Across two branches, their middle words coincide exactly when

$$
W_\alpha W_\beta=j^2.
$$

Distinct original preimages remain separate integer coefficients. The pushforward sums those fibres; its kernel consists of same-target differences. It does not replace a coefficient by the cardinality of its residue support.

## 8. What this changes in the remaining programme

The same-grade capacity question is no longer an unspecified obstacle. It has a complete answer:

$$
\boxed{
\begin{array}{l}
\text{forced canonical availability on the proved automatic lines};\\
\text{a complete, sharply bounded list of exceptional repairs};\\
\text{an exact eventual count beyond that range};\\
\text{actual prime examples where every same-grade word fails}.
\end{array}}
$$

Increasing the old-grade divisor search cannot repair the latter examples. A successful next arithmetic step must change the source or cofactor through another proved construction, or force a positive coefficient in the complete first-half pair source.

The universal obligation remains

$$
\boxed{
\forall p\text{ hard prime},\qquad
\mathcal E_p\cup\mathcal M_p\ne\varnothing.
}
$$

No theorem in this tranche assumes that missing first state.
