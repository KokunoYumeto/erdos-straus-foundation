\title{A Genuine Three-Support Class in an ES Divisor Source}
\author{The Clankers}

\begin{abstract}
Actual residue-pushforward kernels on the nine divisors of $118^2$ give a
mixed cohomology class invisible on every pair of supports. We calculate its
complete integral representatives, local-gluing obstruction and original
quotient metric. The construction is an explicit arithmetic application of
support-indexed internal homology, not a reinterpretation of a three-column
Gram matrix. It does not prove universal Erd\H{o}s--Straus existence.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Source framework.}
The SplitZero reconstruction retains a diagram of modules on a
join-semilattice and takes its disjoint union: supported zero $(S,0)$ is
not absence $\tau$. Quotients use the actual incoming differential; a
transition kills a class precisely when its chain image has a receiving
boundary primitive \cite[\S\S1--4]{SZ}. Here supports are subsets of three
original relation modules. Inclusions of supports include their actual
intersection complexes. An additional bottom is the zero complex; the
empty test set still carries $V$. The inclusions, differentials and induced homology transitions are coherent
linear families and have their SplitZero reconstructions. Individual choices
of sections are not assumed natural; their relation corrections are retained.

\begin{theorem}[Mixed relation and local-gluing comparison]
Let $U_1,U_2,U_3$ be submodules of a module $V$ over a commutative ring $k$.
In degrees $-3,-2,-1,0$ form
\[
 C:\quad U_{123}\longrightarrow U_{12}\oplus U_{13}\oplus U_{23}
 \longrightarrow U_1\oplus U_2\oplus U_3\longrightarrow V,
\]
where $U_I=\bigcap_{i\in I}U_i$ and
\[
 d_3(t)=(t,-t,t),\quad d_2(a,b,c)=(-a-b,a-c,b+c),\quad
 d_1(u_1,u_2,u_3)=u_1+u_2+u_3.
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{H^{-1}(C)=
 \frac{U_3\cap(U_1+U_2)}{U_{13}+U_{23}},\quad
 H^{-2}(C)=H^{-3}(C)=0,\quad H^0(C)=V/(U_1+U_2+U_3).}       \tag{1}
\]
The first isomorphism sends a cycle to its third coordinate. Let
\[
 \mathcal G=\{([v_i])\in\textstyle\bigoplus_iV/U_i:
                         v_j-v_i\in U_i+U_j\ (i<j)\}.
\]
There is also the exact comparison
\[
 \boxed{H^{-1}(C)\simeq\mathcal G/\operatorname{diag}V,
 \qquad[(u_1,u_2,u_3)]\longmapsto[(0,u_3,0)].}              \tag{2}
\]
Every two-submodule restriction has $H^{-1}=0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For (1), a cycle's third coordinate lies in the stated numerator.
A boundary changes it by $b+c$. An element $u_3$ of that numerator lifts
by choosing $-u_3=u_1+u_2$; two choices differ by $(v,-v,0)$,
$v\in U_{12}$. If $u_3=b+c$, subtract $d_2(0,b,c)$; the remaining
cycle is of this same pair-boundary form. Also $\ker d_2$ consists of
$(t,-t,t)$ with $t\in U_{123}$ and $d_3$ is injective. This proves all
groups and inverse fibres, including the pair case.
For (2), adding $d_2(a,b,c)$ changes the middle quotient by $b+c$.
The term $c$ vanishes modulo $U_2$ and $(0,b,0)$ is diagonal since
$b\in U_1\cap U_3$. To invert a compatible triple, subtract its first
representative diagonally, then write it as $(0,a_1,b_1)$ with
$a_1,b_1\in U_1$. Write $b_1-a_1=c_2+c_3$ with $c_i\in U_i$.
Subtracting $b_1$ gives $(0,-c_3,0)$, the image of the cycle
$(b_1-a_1,-c_2,-c_3)$. If a cycle image is diagonal $v$, then $v\in U_{13}$ and
$u_3-v\in U_{23}$, so (1) makes it a boundary. This proves injectivity and
the inverse choices. The diagonal map has its own kernel $U_{123}$.
\end{proof}
The intersection-complex and distributive-lattice context are classical
\cite[\S2.1, (5)--(8); \S3]{Mas}. No distributivity is presumed in (1).

\paragraph{The exact arithmetic modules.}
Take the prime $p=241$ and its first-half ES shell
\[
 a=118=2\cdot59,\qquad R=4a-p=231=3\cdot7\cdot11.
\]
Take $k$ nontrivial. The original divisor set, in its retained order, is
\[
 \mathcal D=\{1,2,4,59,118,236,3481,6962,13924\},\quad V=k^{\mathcal D}.
\]
For $\ell=3,7,11$ use $r_\ell(e_u)=e_{u\bmod\ell}$ and set
$U_1=\ker r_3,U_2=\ker r_7,U_3=\ker r_{11}$.
Every residue fibre has reference $u_0$ and complete relation basis
$e_u-e_{u_0}$. Its coefficient-sum quotient has inverse representative
$e_{u_0}$; the entire inverse fibre adds those relations.
The actual fibres are
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
3&\{1,4,118,3481,13924\},\ \{2,59,236,6962\}\\
7&\{1,13924\},\ \{2,3481\},\ \{4,6962\},\ \{59\},\ \{118\},\ \{236\}\\
11&\{1\},\ \{2\},\ \{4,59\},\ \{118\},\ \{236,3481\},\ \{6962\},\ \{13924\}.
\end{array}                                                   \tag{3}
\]
Set
\[
\begin{gathered}
 a_0=e_{13924}-e_1,\quad v=e_{3481}-e_2,\quad w=e_{6962}-e_4,
 \quad b_0=v+w,\\
 r=e_{59}-e_4,\quad s=e_{3481}-e_{236},\quad c_0=r+s.
\end{gathered}
\]
The complete integral calculations from (3) are
\[
 U_2=\langle a_0,v,w\rangle,\quad U_3=\langle r,s\rangle,
\quad U_{12}=\langle a_0,b_0\rangle,\quad U_{13}=\langle c_0\rangle,
\quad U_{23}=U_{123}=0.                                    \tag{4}
\]
Indeed $a_0$ has zero modulo-three sum, $v,w$ have opposite such sums,
and so do $r,s$. Membership in $U_2$ forces zero coefficients at both
$59$ and $236$; this proves the last intersection is zero. All pivots are
units, so the assertions hold over any commutative $k$.
The seven internal modulo-three fibre differences, together with $v$,
generate every total-sum-zero vector. Hence $H^0(C)=k$.

\begin{theorem}[A nonzero ternary class and its full inverse]
For (3)--(4), all pairwise mixed groups vanish, but
\[
 \boxed{H^{-1}(C)\simeq k,\qquad\gamma=(-v-r,v,r)\text{ generates it}.} \tag{5}
\]
Every cycle has a unique expression
\[
 z=\lambda\gamma+d_2(Aa_0+Bb_0,Cc_0,0),                   \tag{6}
\]
with inverse
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \lambda=[e_{59}]z_3-[e_{3481}]z_3,\quad C=[e_{3481}]z_3,\\
 A=[e_{13924}](z_2-\lambda v),\qquad
 B=[e_{3481}](z_2-\lambda v).
\end{gathered}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $z_3$ in the complete basis $r,s$. Subtract the displayed multiple
of $\gamma$ and the $Cc_0$ boundary; the third component becomes zero.
The remaining second component lies in $U_{12}$ and has the unique
$a_0,b_0$ coefficients shown. This proves existence and uniqueness in
(6), so no nonzero multiple of $\gamma$ is a boundary. Formula (1) gives
the pair statement. This is an integral proof, not just a rational rank test.
\end{proof}
Under (2), the resulting nonzero local-gluing class has representatives
$(0,r,0)$. Its three pairs have common lifts $v+r,0,r$, respectively.
A global lift would be $A(r+s)$, since it must lie in $U_1\cap U_3$.
Its modulo-seven coordinate at residue three forces $A=1$, and that at
residue five forces $A=0$. Thus no global linear lift exists.

\paragraph{Original relation metric and canonical representative.}
Over $\mathbb Q$ or $\mathbb R$, keep the counting metric on each of the
three original copies of $V$. Define the actual incoming columns
\[
 B_0=d_2(a_0,0,0),\quad B_1=d_2(b_0,0,0),\quad B_2=d_2(0,c_0,0).
\]
Direct coefficient multiplication gives
\[
 \boxed{B^*B=\begin{pmatrix}4&0&0\\0&8&2\\0&2&8\end{pmatrix},\quad
 B^*\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}0\\5\\5\end{pmatrix},\quad
 \|\gamma\|^2=8.}                                        \tag{7}
\]
Thus the unchanged source metric gives the least-norm representative
\[
 \boxed{\gamma_h=\gamma-\tfrac12B_1-\tfrac12B_2,
 \quad\|\gamma_h\|^2=3,
 \quad\|\gamma-\gamma_h\|^2=5.}                           \tag{8}
\]
Multiplying (7) by $(0,1/2,1/2)^\mathsf T$ verifies orthogonality.
Every representative of $\lambda[\gamma]$ is $\lambda\gamma_h+Bt$ with
exact squared norm $3\lambda^2+t^*(B^*B)t$. The actual primitive of the
removed term is $(b_0/2,c_0/2,0)$ in degree $-2$. Its source value is not
replaced by zero before passing to the quotient.
This implements the original-metric minimum-section identity of the
SplitZero workbench \cite[AMT7--10]{AMT} on the explicit arithmetic modules.
It does not transfer the workbench's analytic Gamma metric to this example.

\paragraph{The arithmetic target map is additional data.}
Neither $H^0(C)=k$ nor $H^{-1}(C)=k$ is a count of ES solutions.
The preceding residue kernels forget coefficient distributions, whereas
the ES channels require target residues of actual original divisors.
In E the local targets are $-4^{-1}\bmod\ell$; in M they are
$-a\bmod\ell$. With paired standard bases, $r_\ell^*$ pulls each target
delta back to its indicator $p_{\ell,c}$ on $\mathcal D$.
The actual global gate is pointwise product
\[
 \mathbf1_{R\mid g_c(u)}=\prod_{\ell\mid R}p_{\ell,c}(u),
 \quad g_E=4u+1,\quad g_M=u+a.                            \tag{9}
\]
Both channels are empty at this shell. Every local observation and every
higher class above still exists. Formula (9) explicitly compares the source
maps with the arithmetic predicates, rather than pretending their different
cohomologies are one object. For general prime powers one retains every
threshold $\ell^j\mid g_c$ and uses their joint test on the same $u$.

For the separate coordinate failure ideals $B_t=(1-p_t)V$, the complete
intersection complex has basis $(u,I)$ with $I$ a set of failed tests.
Its boundary removes one test with alternating sign. Each nonempty failed
set is an augmented simplex, contracted integrally by wedging its least
vertex; only states satisfying every test contribute $H^0$. The larger
workbench tranche proves the split-occurrence double complex, its natural
prime-jet comparison, all higher truncation errors and its uniformly bounded
Hilbert homotopy. These are not assumed in (1)--(9).

\paragraph{Reproduction and status.}
The standard-library verifier reconstructs all fibres, kernels and
intersections, (5)--(8), and the local-gluing rank obstruction. It also checks
625 complete cycle-coordinate returns and both arithmetic gates. A separate
proof is supplied for each claimed universal algebraic identity; finite tests
are not used as substitutes. The general mixed intersection mechanism is
classical \cite{Mas}. The explicit divisor-residue example and its retained
metric calculation are the present application; no historical-priority,
Lean-build, independent-review or universal ES claim is made.
