## Mathematical antecedents

Christian Elsholtz and Terence Tao's *Counting the number of solutions to the
Erdős–Straus equation on unit fractions*, J. Aust. Math. Soc. 94 (2013),
50–105, [arXiv:1107.1010](https://arxiv.org/abs/1107.1010), is the literature
antecedent for the type-I/type-II arithmetic and is cited in the received
papers, especially their use of Section 2. These are not ideas first supplied
by this compilation.

The integral monodromy matrices are taken through the received source from
the circulated *(3,4,infinity) modular family of 2-tori* manuscript hosted by
Levent Alpöge at [the manuscript source](https://alpo.ge/s6.pdf). That
circulation is not an attribution of all its authorship to the host. The
received bibliography also records Philip Engel's exposition and its
authorship discussion. The new algebraic proofs use the displayed matrices,
not the manuscript's claimed global six-sphere conclusion.

Jeroen Sijsling and John Voight, *On computing Belyi maps*, Pub. Math.
Besançon 2014/1, 73–131,
[arXiv:1311.2529](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.2529), is the cited cover and
permutation-triple antecedent. The local branch correction here gives its
own coordinate proof. Cyclotomic factorization, finite character
orthogonality, quadratic reciprocity and the Chinese remainder theorem are
classical inputs; this edition does not claim to originate them.

Mark Blunk, *Del Pezzo Surfaces of degree 6 over an arbitrary field*,
[arXiv:0805.0119v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/0805.0119v2), Section 2,
Proposition 2.1 (printed page 3), supplies the primary reference for the
six boundary curves, their intersection pairing, and the split toric model.
That section was read directly. The marked contraction and coordinate action
in the new reader give the precise comparison used in the joint discussion;
the classical surface model is not presented as a new invention here.
