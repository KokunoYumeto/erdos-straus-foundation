{\LARGE\bfseries Every integer triple has\par a shortest Leech lift}\par\vspace{8pt}
Three disjoint Golay octads give a trace map $P:\Lambda\to\mathbb Z^3$. For every $t\in\mathbb Z^3$,
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\min_{P\lambda=t}\|\lambda\|^2
=\frac{\|t\|^2}{4}+\mu(t\bmod4).}
\]
Take $r_i\in\{0,1,2,3\}$ and reduce $3$ to $-1$, giving $a(r)$. Then
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\mu(r)=\begin{cases}
0&r=(0,0,0),\\
3&r=(2,2,2),\\
4-\|a(r)\|^2/4&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}}
\]
In particular, the extra squared-norm cost is always at most $15/4$.

\textbf{A finite seed proof, not a height extrapolation.} Eight explicit norm-four seeds, code-preserving phase permutations and block sign changes give all 62 ordinary nonzero residues. A balanced dodecad realizes norm six at $(2,2,2)$, and parity proves that lower bound. Zero handles the remaining class.

Let $w_i=2\mathbf1_{B_i}/\sqrt8$. Translation changes the $i$th trace by four and leaves the perpendicular component fixed. Hence
\[
\lambda_*(t)=\nu_r+\sum_i\frac{t_i-a_i(r)}4w_i
\]
is an explicit shortest inverse for every integer input.

The entire fibre is $\sigma(t)+\ker P$. Keeping that kernel preserves the marked state under subsequent maps; selecting a shortest representative does not remove the other states.

\textit{Full statement: An eight-seed proof of the shortest three-octad Leech trace inverse.}
