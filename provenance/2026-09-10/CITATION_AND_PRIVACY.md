# Citing removed sources without erasing contributions

This is a proposed PolyClank editorial convention, not a legal ruling or permission to republish somebody else's work.

## Preserve historical causation; update mathematical reliance

A work can be a genuine source of an idea even if it is later withdrawn, corrected, deleted, or found not to be novel. Record its role as an initiating observation or source lead. For the mathematical fact itself, cite an appropriate reliable source or an independently supplied proof. Do not use a withdrawn argument as an unqualified premise.

COPE's [retraction guidance](https://doi.org/10.24318/cope.2019.1.4) explains that retraction corrects the literature rather than punishes authors, and calls for clearly identified notices linked to the affected article. [arXiv's withdrawal policy](https://info.arxiv.org/help/withdraw.html) keeps earlier announced versions accessible while marking the withdrawal. [Zenodo's record-management guide](https://help.zenodo.org/docs/deposit/manage-records/) describes tombstones containing citations. [DataCite's DOI-persistence guidance](https://support.datacite.org/docs/doi-persistence) retains registered identifiers and permits redirection to tombstones. These policies support identifiable history; they do not warrant treating the withdrawn result as correct.

For a formal retraction, identify the exact version and notice and explain why the citation is retained. For an unavailable source, record what was actually inspected and when, and separate direct evidence from a later report about it. Do not invent a reason for removal.

## A deleted Reddit post is not automatically a retracted paper

Deletion does not by itself establish error, misconduct, embarrassment, or withdrawal of a specific mathematical claim. For the initiating ES post, retain a restrained historical reference with the original public handle and source URL, mark the reported deletion, and state that credit concerns prompting the investigation, not novelty or endorsement of subsequent work. Do not repost the deleted body or seek an offline identity.

A courteous request for the preferred attribution is appropriate when feasible, especially after a deletion. Silence is not permission to republish the body or private communications. If the contributor requests less identifying attribution, review the live contribution record and use a non-identifying source reference where appropriate rather than assign their contribution to someone else. Historical integrity and privacy both require judgment; neither is a license to expose private aliases.
