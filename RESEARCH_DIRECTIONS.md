This page preserves questions, motivations and partial constructions, not a compulsory task queue. A direction can be valuable independently of solving ES. [RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md) records separately usable outputs; these programmes do not automatically inherit their status.

The owner, GitHub **KokunoYumeto** and Reddit **u/lepthymo**, supplied the current motivation account. ## 1. Star–Kneser interaction

The owner's star/snowflake suggestion concerned how divisor support and a weighted target jointly force a hit, not merely extending a numerical sieve.

The archive contains three distinct TeX manuscripts with PDFs: The Star-Kneser Defect Theorem (19 pages), A Star–Kneser Positivity Criterion (16 pages), and Star–Kneser Positivity (17 pages). Their title pages say May 2026. They develop the weighted target, six Fourier components, split-zero support and a quotient obstruction. Here Kneser is the addition theorem for finite abelian groups; these files have not been established as the exact versions lost through the chat interface.

R09 reconstructs the finite argument. The missing application is a proof of the required positive surplus across the relevant arithmetic inputs. The criterion can also be studied for other finite support families. Source IDs: CN-f16db60da4a3e3, CN-3f8d61d88e2c66, CN-cd08053554f277.

## 2. Residual fibration, Niemeier data and Leech questions

The owner pursued A₅⁴D₄ and the Leech lattice because explicit correspondences could make established lattice knowledge transferable back to arithmetic.

CN-679a329c81c154, lines 657–739, describes the modulo-840 fibration, four A₅ augmentation lattices, a D₄ component and order-72 duad/tetracode glue. Lines 741–861 discuss the umbral row, characters, eta product, code layer and modular comparisons; lines 885–898 concern Leech deep-hole context. R10 reconstructs and checks the finite glue data, not every later correspondence.

The unfinished question is which arithmetic data survive these constructions and which lattice or modular conclusions return through an actual map. A shared Coxeter number is not that map; failure of one proposed map does not prove the fields unrelated.

## 3. Three colours and the number 72

The current owner statement asks for a three-colour refinement rather than an automatic reduction to a two-component comparison. A completed old implementation of that exact proposal was not identified in the decoded text. The order-72 glue above is not dimension 72.

R08 performs a new explicit test: three permuted copies of a rank-d lattice, diagonal and zero-sum sublattices, index 3^d and an Eisenstein action on the zero-sum part. A Leech input gives real rank 72 and minimum 4 in the ambient direct sum.

What remains is to determine whether the intended colours specify copies, a grading, a module structure or a nontrivial glue, and which choice has an arithmetic origin. Nebe's arXiv:1008.2862 and Shimada's arXiv:2311.18309 are new primary-literature leads, not retroactively assigned sources of the original idea.

## 5. Split zero and the proposed number universes

The motivating question separates absence from cancellation. The more ambitious “universes across a second zero” idea is an open programme, not a ready-made synonym for a standard object.

The Star–Kneser source constructs a ring with an additional element τ. CN-239f20d6f2e964 contains a much larger split-support/projective programme with poles, limits, modal identities and curve comparisons. These are different versions and levels of formalization.

R01 proves precise algebraic laws for one construction. The larger programme needs explicit operations, return maps and support semantics. A support label does not itself force a nonempty arithmetic support. Novelty is not inferred from the terminology.

## 6. Busy Beaver, Kolmogorov complexity and volume

The public thread records that a Kolmogorov-complexity video prompted the owner to compare 107 with the four-state Busy Beaver runtime and to investigate the five-state value. The current recollection adds complexity–volume as the bridge they wanted explored.

CN-7bae3791cc8514 gives exact congruences involving 6,21,107,47,176,870 and the ES record case. CN-56a02a22fb3296 develops arithmetic reconstruction; CN-4d668c84ad396a distinguishes finite congruences from a complexity conjecture. R04 preserves the actual identities.

A completed Kolmogorov-complexity or complexity–volume theorem was not recovered from the decoded text. That search does not exclude other images, binaries or missing conversations. The continuing question requires specified notions of machine description, runtime, output/support and volume, followed by an explicit map and preservation statement. Runtime, printed-symbol count and space usage are not interchangeable statistics.

R05 reconstructs those facts and derives a common next-odd-Collatz-value relation. This does not attribute the new formulation retroactively to CivQ17 or claim convergence. Separate Collatz/automata drafts CN-6d25f4720ad4a3 and CN-1cfd281002ed14 were indexed but not fully audited.

A Collatz workbench using this recurrence should retain the contribution link. Repository searches did not positively identify a current Collatz repository or session. This page is therefore the handoff, not a claim that another session was contacted.

## 10. Correction history and safe continuation

The owner reports an earlier endpoint overclaim withdrawn after u/UmbrellaCorp_HR exposed an assumed key implication. The archive also groups some drafts as overstated. This pass did not identify the exact withdrawn DOI/version and defective line. Such material is a correction-history lead, not an accepted dependency.

Preserve the distinction between an invalid proof, a false subsidiary statement and an unresolved theorem. A repaired argument receives no inherited approval. No personal medical history belongs in this public mathematical record.

Each continuing agent should see what was tried, why, what was obtained and what remains unresolved. It is free to pursue another route. It must neither silently discard a requested correspondence nor describe an unproved implication as a completed result.
